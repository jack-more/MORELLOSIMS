# The YOJO set

## The call

Make one GHK-Cu hero. Everything else is there to make the copper work better.

A second GHK-Cu bottle would compete with the hero, so copper stays in the egg.

The set is built from what the research supports (see "Evidence" below), not from what looks good in a lineup.

## Set of 4

| Step | Product | Shape | Color | Why it belongs next to GHK-Cu |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Cleanse | Cleansing bar on a rope, with a Y inside | Frosted pebble | Frost | Must be a **syndet** bar at pH 5.5–7. True soap is about pH 10, raises skin pH, and alkaline conditions are where GHK-Cu breaks down most. |
| 2 Hydrate | Hyaluronic serum (HA plus panthenol, optional centella) | Dropper with a knurled chrome collar | Chalk Rose | Layers under the cream with no chemical interaction. Brand practice: NIOD tells customers to follow its copper serum with its HA product. |
| 3 Treat | **YOJO GHK-CU cream** | The egg | Chalk Sky | The hero. Use AM and PM. |
| 4 Protect | Daily SPF | Tube with a knurled chrome cap | Chalk Sage | Every copper-peptide regimen we checked (The Ordinary, Medicube, Naturium) ends with SPF in the morning. Zinc oxide is routinely formulated alongside copper peptides. |

## Set of 3

The bar, the serum and the cream.

This is the launch set, because all three are cosmetics. SPF is the fourth piece once it's ready, because in the US any SPF claim makes the product an OTC drug under FDA Monograph M020. That means a monograph-compliant manufacturer, drug listing and SPF testing.

## Cut from the core set

- **Lip product:** nothing links it to GHK-Cu, so it's an add-on. Use it as a gift-with-purchase or a drop.
- **JELLY water-gel:** a second moisturizer that overlaps the cream. It could come back later as a lighter alternative to the cream.
- **Cica tube:** fine to use with copper, but centella can go into the serum instead of being its own product.

## Never in the YOJO line

- **L-ascorbic acid (vitamin C):** the strongest conflict.
  - It reduces Cu(II) to Cu(I), which can produce free radicals.
  - Its pH (~3) is outside GHK-Cu's stable range.
  - If customers use vitamin C, the guidance is vitamin C in the morning and copper at night.
- **AHA/BHA acids:** they lower the pH and add irritation. Use them on a different night.
- **Retinoids:** no chemistry shows they inactivate GHK-Cu, but irritation stacks. The Ordinary says not to combine them. Alternate nights.
- **Benzoyl peroxide:** a strong oxidiser. Use it at a different time of day.
- **Acne claims:** any acne claim makes a product an OTC drug (Monograph M006).

## Formulation notes for the manufacturer

- Finished pH 5–7. GHK-Cu was stable at pH 4.5–7.4 in a 2016 stability study.
- Add the peptide last, below 40 °C.
- No EDTA or other chelators in the GHK-Cu formula, because they pull the copper out.
- No fruit acids and no ionic plant extracts (e.g., aloe) in the same formula.

## Evidence

- No peer-reviewed study tests GHK-Cu against specific co-actives on human skin.
- These rules come from copper chemistry, supplier formulation notes and brand labels.
- The clinical GHK-Cu trials are small 8–12 week studies, and most of the reviews are written by the people who commercialised it.
- The research was compiled on 2026-10-28.
