# Build ZUMA's ownership on purchases, not holdings

The protocols with durable grassroots ownership did not get it from clever token mechanics. They got it because the action they rewarded was worth doing without the reward, and because they made a few verifiable promises and kept them. Hyperliquid sent 31% of its supply, fully unlocked, to users who had paid real trading fees on a product that already worked. It took no VC money and has directed fees to "the community" ever since. Jito's points rewarded a better staking product and deposits kept growing after the airdrop. Blur, Blast, Parcl, Friend.tech and the brand NFT programs rewarded cheap or speculative actions, and their activity collapsed once the rewards stopped. For ZUMA the lesson cuts both ways. Paying people for delivered, tested orders and for referrals of genuine customers is the right foundation, and the lot-report fingerprints on-chain are ZUMA's version of Hyperliquid's checkable claims. Two parts of the current design point the wrong way, though, and both should change before launch. The first is the **holdings-based referral multiplier**, where a larger $ZUMA balance pays more USDC per referral. It is the weakest point under Howey, it reads to the FTC as pay-for-higher-commission, and it concentrates rewards in whale wallets. The second is the **voting streak's referral boost**, which pays cash-linked commissions for clicking a ballot. The recommended plan has five parts. Earn referral levels from verified referred customers rather than token balance. Confine any token-linked kicker to store credit. Cut the streak to a smaller, decaying discount with no referral component. Make the factory vote one verified member, one vote, from a QA-vetted shortlist that the company has final say over. Drop "treasury buybacks" from the launch materials entirely. None of this is legal advice, and the token, the multipliers and the peptide marketing all need securities and FDA counsel before launch.

## Rewards held only where the rewarded action was worth doing anyway

Hyperliquid is the reference case because it combined product-market fit with an unusually clean distribution. Its points ran at a fixed **1M points a week from November 2023 to May 2024, then 700K a week**. Referrers earned 1 point for every 4 points their referees earned. The criteria were deliberately vague, and the team reserved the right to revise past distributions ([Hyperliquid Docs: Points](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/points.md)). At genesis on November 29, 2024, **310M HYPE (31%) went to points holders fully unlocked**. There was "no allocations for private investors, centralized exchanges, or market makers", and core contributors got 23.8% under a one-year lock ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/328631/hyperliquid-plans-to-launch-hype-token-in-nov-29-genesis-event)). Fees are "entirely directed to the community (HLP, the assistance fund, and deployers)" ([Hyperliquid Docs: Fees](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees.md)). DefiLlama put annualized revenue at about $691M in October 2026 ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/hyperliquid)). The ownership culture rests on facts anyone can check, such as the genesis table and the fee destination. Hyperliquid also handled its biggest trust risk, team unlocks, with predictability: monthly tranches on the 6th of each month ([Netcoins](https://netcoins.com/blog/hyperliquids-hype-token-unlocks)). It has failures too. Thin-market manipulation cost its community vault about **$4M (ETH whale), $12–13.5M (JELLY) and $4.5–4.9M (POPCAT)**, and the JELLY fix was a validator vote settling at a price favorable to the vault, which critics called centralized intervention ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/348314/hyperliquid-delists-jellyjelly-memecoin-amid-whale-manipulation-fiasco); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/large-popcat-trades-result-in-usd5-million-loss-for-hyperliquid-vault)). The node software was still closed source as of July 2026 ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/who-actually-runs-hyperliquid-the-governance-audit/)). Its legitimacy came from outcomes that favored holders, not from procedural decentralization. That arrangement lasts only as long as the outcomes stay favorable.

The points programs that failed show what the reward was really buying. **Blast's TVL fell from $2.3B to about $875M within weeks of its June 2024 airdrop** ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/blast-struggles-after-its-highly-anticipated-airdrop)). Parcl lost 74% of TVL and saw daily users fall from over 10k to about 500, while Kamino's daily users fell to about 3k. In the same study Jito was an "extreme outlier", with JitoSOL stake up 63% after the drop because the product was good on its own ([Tiger Research / Delphi](https://reports.tiger-research.com/p/do-airdrops-hurt-more-than-they-help-eng)). Blur had zero fees and zero royalties, so nothing but gas stood between farmers and points. CryptoSlam flagged at least **$577M of Blur-linked sales as wash trades** ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/at-least-us577-million-blur-linked-nft-sales-wash-trades-cryptoslam-says/)). Backlash came when opaque formulas met a disappointing conversion. EigenLayer's linear allocation gave an estimated **90% of the drop to the top 2% of stakers**, and it had to add minimum allocations after the outcry ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/eigenlayer-airdrop-additional-eigen-token-days-after-backlash)).

Social tokens make the same point more sharply. Friend.tech earned about **$90M in lifetime fees**, then handed its contracts to a null address in September 2024 with deposits down 92%. Its revenue came from speculation on keys, not from use of a service ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/friend-tech-shuts-down-after-revenue-and-users-plummet/)). Its V2 airdrop made users follow 10 accounts and join a club to claim. Farmers did both and sold, and FRIEND fell 46% in four hours ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/markets/friend-tech-token-tanks-after-shambled-debut)). Farcaster grew without a token, using credits and USDC creator rewards. It still stalled and was sold to Neynar in January 2026, with $180M returned to investors ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/386816/farcaster-repays-venture-backers)). Brand loyalty tokens failed where the token was the product: Starbucks Odyssey closed in March 2024 ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/quick-service/starbucks-shutters-odyssey-its-nft-backed-metaverse-program)), Nike wound down RTFKT and now faces a securities class action ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/294410/nike-killing-nft-sneaker-rtfkt)), and Reddit ended Moons citing regulation, after which **MOON fell over 80% amid insider-selling allegations** ([Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/markets/cryptocurrency/23/10/35303061/reddit-is-phasing-out-crypto-powered-community-points-reward-tokens-moon-plunges-over-80)). The patterns that held up hid the chain and offered a plain commerce perk: Reddit's card-bought avatars with 3M+ Vault wallets ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2427464)), Shopify token-gated discounts ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/token-gating)) and Coinbase One's fee-free subscription ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/coinbase-one-launches-zero-fees)). On Solana, the Boba Guys passport pilot reported +65% monthly spend with a non-speculative reward, though the figures are vendor-reported ([Solana](https://solana.com/news/case-study-boba-guys)).

DePIN adds the clearest model of what to reward: verified real-world work, weighted by usefulness. Hivemapper weights mapper rewards by coverage, freshness and quality, so contributors drive the roads the map needs ([Hivemapper docs](https://docs.hivemapper.com/honey-token/earning-honey/individual-reward-factors)). Helium shows the cost of a weak link to physical proof. About **5% of hotspots were denylisted as fraudulent**, and HIP-66 admitted that "spoofers are still thriving" ([Medium, Tobback](https://medium.com/@tomtobback/the-helium-denylist-going-after-the-scammers-maybe-part1-613b0a8af89c); [HIP-66](https://github.com/helium/HIP/blob/main/0066-trust-score-and-denylist-convenience.md)). In January 2026 its "$0 Zero plan" began charging fees, which early adopters called a betrayal ([BlockEden forum](https://blockeden.xyz/forum/t/heliums-data-credit-burns-doubled-to-361k-month-at-t-integrated-its-network-but-the-zero-plan-now-has-fees-is-depin-telecom-growing-up-or-selling-out/883)). The parallel for ZUMA is direct. A delivered, non-refunded, tested order is ZUMA's proof-of-coverage, and a third-party lab's signed report is its quality score.

| Protocol | What worked | What failed | ZUMA verdict |
|---|---|---|---|

## Every value-accrual mechanism studied is the one ZUMA cannot build

The second lesson is that the "real utility" most protocols advertise is a claim on revenue, which is the opposite of what ZUMA is allowed to offer. Curve's veCRV pays lockers fees and lets them direct emissions. That turned votes into a priced commodity: in one week more than $18M of bribes flowed through Votium, and **each $1 of bribes steered about $2.05 of CRV emissions** ([Incentivized](https://incentivized.substack.com/p/how-bribes-drive-the-curve-wars)). Academic work finds Votium markets largely decide Convex's gauge votes ([arXiv 2311.17589](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.17589)). Aerodrome pays voters 100% of the fees from the pools they vote for ([Paragraph](https://paragraph.com/@defi--world/how-aerodromes-veaero-system-actually-works-1)), which is revenue share in its purest form. Aave approved buybacks in April 2025, then cut the budget from $50M to $30M when fees fell about 25%, a pro-cyclical pattern a small shop would feel more sharply (aggregator-sourced; [Phemex](https://phemex.com/blogs/aave-surging-automated-buyback-engine)). Uniswap took years to pass its fee switch, finally burning 100M UNI in December 2025 ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/uniswap-passes-unification-fee-switch-proposal)). Ethena gated its buybacks behind a $7.5B USDe milestone it had not reached by late September 2026 ([CryptoTicker](https://cryptoticker.io/en/ethena-fee-switch-ena-buyback/)). Pump.fun is the cautionary end state. It sent 100% of revenue to buybacks and then burned about $370M of PUMP in April 2026, and the token still sat **about 84% below its all-time high** under unlock pressure ([Bitcoin.com](https://news.bitcoin.com/pump-fun-burns-370-million-pump-tokens-revenue-buyback/)). Jupiter's Litterbox trust bought 29.5M JUP in Q3 2026 alone, alongside its burns and paused emissions ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/jupiter-quarterly-report-q3-2026)).

Even Hyperliquid's own wording carries a lesson. Its docs describe the Assistance Fund as converting fees to HYPE "in a fully automated manner" and say the HYPE "is burned", never as a buyback or a return, even though the press calls it a buyback ([Hyperliquid Docs: Fees](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees.md)). For a US-facing shop, softer wording does not fix the underlying problem. A sales-funded burn is still value accrual. The SEC's 2017 Munchee order found a "utility" token to be a security partly because the issuer touted burns and scarcity ([SEC Order 33-10445](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/admin/2017/33-10445.pdf)). ZUMA's current site preview lists "Treasury buybacks: Under legal review. Not part of launch, with nothing promised" as a step in how $ZUMA works. That line should come out. Listing it as an option on the roadmap is itself a representation that holders will read as a future price lever.

Governance is the other trap. Low turnout lets coordinated blocs capture votes. Compound's Proposal 289 passed **51.84% to 48.16%**, moving 499,000 COMP to a group-controlled vault using votes partly delegated from exchange-held funds ([CoinDesk](https://coindesk.com/markets/2024/07/29/comp-down-67-after-supposed-governance-attack-on-compound-dao); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/compound-community-accuses-notorious-whale-of-engineering-governance-attack)). MakerDAO's Endgame vote was decided by a founder's economic influence across only 122 addresses ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/makerdao-mkr-endgame-plan-passed-in-a-vote-where-founder-had-60-influence/)). Paying for participation buys participation, not judgment. Arbitrum's delegate incentives raised measured participation 12–13% while subjective-scoring disputes grew ([Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/dip-v1-6-from-engagement-to-rewards-a-comprehensive-analysis-of-the-delegate-incentives-program/29475)). Optimism admits Retro Funding "lacks sufficient evidence that rewards have caused significant ecosystem growth" and is shifting toward expert and metric-based evaluation ([Optimism](https://optimism.io/blog/retro-funding-2025)). Jupiter paid **50M JUP a quarter** to stakers who voted. A forum commenter conceded the scheme "may not incentivize informed voting but will get people to participate" ([Jupiter forum](https://discuss.jup.ag/t/asr-active-staking-rewards-methodology/19338)), and in June 2025 the DAO paused voting, citing "a breakdown in trust" and "the perpetual FUD cycle" ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/06/20/solana-dex-jupiter-pauses-dao-votes-citing-breakdown-in-trust)). ZUMA's voting streak is a small copy of Jupiter's paid-voting scheme. Its factory vote also has a feature most DAO votes lack: a third party, the factories, earns money from the outcome. Curve's history shows that such votes get priced and bought.

## The holdings multiplier is ZUMA's weakest legal and economic point

US law gives ZUMA a usable lane that is easy to leave. The SEC–CFTC interpretive release of **March 17, 2026** (effective March 23) places "digital tools" outside the securities definition. These are memberships, tickets and credentials that lack "intrinsic economic properties or rights, such as generating a passive yield or conveying rights to future income, profits, or assets". The release compares holders to people "acquiring a museum membership" ([SEC release 33-11412](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2026/33-11412.pdf)). It also warns that "even a non-security crypto asset may be offered as part of an investment contract", and says that attributable, specific representations on websites and social channels drive the analysis ([Dechert](https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2026/3/sec-s-crypto-framework--the-new-token-taxonomy.html); [GT Law](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/3/sec-clarifies-status-of-crypto-assets-under-federal-securities-laws-signals-potential-exemptive-and-safe-harbor-framework)). The release is not statute, courts need not defer to it, and a later administration can withdraw it. The CLARITY Act failed cloture **49–50 on September 15, 2026** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/15/senate-cloture-vote-on-clarity-act-fails-dealing-regulatory-setback-to-crypto-industry.html)). The proposed Regulation Crypto Assets exemptions are still open for comment until October 20, 2026 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-76-sec-proposes-new-regulation-crypto-assets)). The closest analog is the November 2025 Fuse no-action letter for a "consumptive rewards system", in which "consumers will earn Tokens for their own consumption" ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/380272/fuse-crypto-receives-sec-no-action-relief-letter-over-its-green-energy-rewards-token)). Member pricing and streak discounts fit that profile.

The current referral ladder does not fit it. ZUMA's spec sets levels by time-weighted $ZUMA balance: 10k for 1.25x, 50k for 1.5x, 250k for 2.0x, 1M for 2.5x and **5M (0.5% of a 1B supply) for 3.0x, or 30% of every referred order in USDC**. The argument against securities status is that the referrer earns from their own efforts. The counter-argument is that a bigger bag produces a bigger cash rate, which invites buying tokens to raise a yield. The regulatory notes identify this as the feature most likely to attract an investment-contract theory. The FTC angle runs the same way. A single-level commission on genuine retail sales is not a pyramid, but making participants buy something to earn a higher commission rate is the pay-to-play element the FTC's MLM guidance flags ([FTC MLM guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guidance-concerning-multi-level-marketing)). The FTC has also sued crypto chain-referral schemes before ([FTC complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/cases/dluca_-_bitcoint_funding_team_complaint.pdf)). The economics are also poor. Hyperliquid pays referrers **10% of fees**, capped at a referee's first $1B of volume ([Hyperliquid Docs: Referrals](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/referrals)). ZUMA pays up to 30% of *order value*, which comes straight out of gross margin. Balance-linked tiers also concentrate rewards: EigenLayer's top 2% took about 90%, and Jupiter commenters cite about 90% of voting power held by about 1% of wallets ([Jupiter forum](https://discuss.jup.ag/t/asr-active-staking-rewards-methodology/19338)).

The product category raises every one of these risks. The FDA sent two batches of warning letters to "research use only" peptide sellers in 2026, dated **March 31 and August 24**. It inferred human-drug intent from "the total context of a seller's website", including bacteriostatic-water bundles sold "as a means to prepare an injectable drug" ([FDA letter 735127](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/peak-performance-peptides-735127-08242026); [ChemVerify](https://www.chemverify.com/learn/fda-research-use-only-enforcement-warning-letters-2026)). GLP-1-class compounds were the focus. BPC-157's April 2026 removal from Category 2 did not make it lawful to sell for human use ([D.J. Holt Law](https://djholtlaw.com/regulatory-alert-the-legal-status-of-bpc-157-in-compounding-and-clinical-practice/)). A token community generates exactly the content FDA reads as intent: referrer posts, Discord threads and governance debates about "which factory's lot works better". Under the FTC Endorsement Guides, ZUMA is liable for its referrers' undisclosed connections and health claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/federal-trade-commission-announces-updated-advertising-guides-combat-deceptive-reviews-endorsements)). Crypto checkout for a category that Stripe lists as restricted also looks like processor evasion ([Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/en-gb-ca/legal/restricted-businesses)). The factory vote carries its own exposure. *Samuels v. Lido DAO* allowed claims that governance participants can be general partners ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/292275/california-court-rules-lido-dao-members-can-be-held-liable-under-partnership-laws)), so the vote should be advisory, with the company holding final authority and legal responsibility for the manufacturing choice.

Payments are lower risk if ZUMA stays a merchant. Under FinCEN's 2019 guidance, a merchant accepting crypto for its own goods is a user, not a money transmitter ([FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf)). California's DFAL, mandatory since July 1, 2026, exempts merchants that accept digital assets, but it is unclear whether that exemption covers *paying out* rewards ([Baker McKenzie](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2026/03/09/californias-crypto-licensing-era-begins-dfpi-commences-acceptance-of-dfal-applications-on-march-9th/)). Closed-loop store credit is therefore the safest payout. USDC referral payouts are acceptable as a marketing expense, but users must never be able to withdraw accumulated credit as USDC. Payout wallets need OFAC screening ([OFAC guidance](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/913571/download?inline)). Referral rewards are ordinary income to recipients, so ZUMA needs W-9s and 1099s above the reporting threshold, which may have changed for 2026. Those rewards fall outside Form 1099-DA ([CoinTracking](https://cointracking.info/blog/form-1099-da/)). An offshore foundation or US geofence does not help. A US-facing store whose token unlocks pricing for US customers cannot geofence the token without geofencing the business, and US regulators have pursued offshore platforms that served US users ([CFTC on Polymarket](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8478-22)).

## Concrete mechanisms ZUMA should run

### Referrals: earn levels from verified customers, not token balance

ZUMA's existing referral spec is already better than most crypto programs. It uses single-level rewards, a 12-month binding that a later code cannot move, 30-day clearing at the lower of two level readings, a lowest-daily-balance rule, self-referral checks across email, wallet, payment and address, and velocity reviews. Keep all of that. The change is what sets the level. Replace the token-balance ladder with **partner levels earned from distinct, attested referred customers whose orders delivered and cleared in a rolling 180 days**. This follows the volume-tier logic Binance uses, with tiers that drop only after sustained underperformance ([Binance Wallet Referral FAQ](https://www.binance.com/learn/binance-wallet-referral-program)). Lower the ceiling too, because ZUMA pays on order value, not fees. The proposed rates below are a starting design, not evidence-derived constants, and should be tested against margin per compound.

| Level | Current rule (token balance) | Current rate | Recommended rule (earned) | Recommended cash rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | <10k $ZUMA | 10% | Any account with one delivered order of its own (earn-in, after Hyperliquid's $10k-volume rule) | 10% |
| 1 | 10k | 12.5% | 3 distinct referred customers cleared | 12% |
| 2 | 50k | 15% | 10 cleared | 14% |
| 3 | 250k | 20% | 25 cleared, plus ID verification | 17% |
| 4 | 1M | 25% | 50 cleared, plus ID verification and a clean compliance record | 20% |
| 5 | 5M | 30% | Removed | — |

If ZUMA wants $ZUMA to play some part in referrals, it should be a **store-credit-only kicker**: up to +3 points paid only as closed-loop credit, read from a 30-day lowest balance, never in USDC and never marketed as "hold more to earn more". This keeps the token on the consumption side, which is the mitigation the regulatory notes suggest. Four abuse controls the spec lacks are worth adding. First, block **reciprocal and closed-loop bindings**: A refers B while B refers A creates a two-person rebate ring that the self-referral checks miss, and referral-graph loop detection is standard e-commerce practice ([Track360](https://www.track360.io/learn/refer-a-friend-program-design/preventing-referral-fraud)). Second, require ID verification once a referrer crosses a payout threshold; ID-gating was the defense that worked against multi-account referral fraud at a crypto lender ([HackerNoon](https://sia.hackernoon.com/the-way-of-the-scammer-what-to-look-out-for-when-running-a-crypto-referral-scheme)). Third, require an #ad/commission disclosure on every referrer post, together with the RUO line. Fourth, write into the terms that referral rewards are forfeited for any human-use, dosing or health claim. That forfeiture rule is the shop's version of HIP-3's slashable deployer stake ([Hyperliquid Docs: HIP-3](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-3-builder-deployed-perpetuals)).

### Builders: capped, approved, on-chain affiliate fees

Hyperliquid's builder codes are its most transferable invention. Front ends earn a per-order fee capped at 0.1% on perps and 1% on spot, which the user approves from their main wallet and can revoke, and per-builder fills are public ([Hyperliquid Docs: Builder codes](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/builder-codes.md)). Phantom alone earned over $20M ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/hyperliquid-the-frontend-wars)). Jupiter's open-source referral program lets integrators attach a fee account to swaps ([Jupiter Developers](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/tool-kits/referral-program)). ZUMA's version is a **"lab partner" code** for integrators that route institutional orders: lab-inventory software, procurement portals and research-supply catalogs. The partner earns a fixed, published share of net order value paid in USDC to an on-chain fee account, with an on-chain record of routed orders. To qualify, a partner posts a **USDC bond** (not $ZUMA, so earning never requires buying the token), which is forfeited for RUO violations. Solana Blinks can carry the checkout link ([Solana Foundation](https://solana.com/it/news/solana-actions-blinks-simplify-transactions-onchain)). This turns distributors into stakeholders without giving them tokens, and it moves acquisition toward lab buyers, the customers whose context best supports the research-use label.

### Lot attestations: make the checkable claim the brand

ZUMA already writes a SHA-256 fingerprint of each lot report on-chain with the lot number. The upgrade is to issue each report as a **Solana Attestation Service credential** under a "ZUMA lot report" schema. The schema would carry the lot number, compound, report hash, test methods (HPLC, MS), test date, an anonymized factory code and the vote round that chose it. Ideally the **independent testing lab co-signs as an authorized issuer**, so the proof does not rest on ZUMA's word alone. SAS has been live on mainnet since May 2025 and is permissionless, and it is built to tie off-chain facts to wallets without putting personal data on-chain ([Solana Foundation](https://solana.com/news/solana-attestation-service)). Compressed NFTs can serve as cheap per-vial receipts, a format proven at Helium's scale ([Solana](https://solana.com/news/case-study-helium-technical-guide)). Attestations should state analytical identity and purity only, never suitability for any use, because FDA reads total context. A failed lot should **automatically disqualify the factory** from the next shortlist. This borrows Aave Umbrella's move from discretionary governance votes to pre-set automatic slashing ([Aave docs: Umbrella](https://aave.com/docs/primitives/umbrella)). It is the most Hyperliquid-like asset ZUMA has: a public, verifiable claim that the company cannot quietly break.

### Factory voting: one verified member, one vote, from a vetted shortlist

Token-weighted voting would let a factory or competitor buy $ZUMA to steer production. That is the Curve bribe dynamic combined with the Compound capture dynamic. ZUMA has something most DAOs lack: verified customers. Use **one vote per attested member**, where eligibility requires a delivered, non-refunded order in the trailing six months plus the researcher attestation ZUMA's store API already issues. Optimism's Citizens' House runs on non-token-weighted badgeholders ([Optimism](https://optimism.io/blog/introducing-the-citizens-house-10m-op-to-public-goods)). Quadratic voting is meaningful only when paired with identity, which is why Gitcoin pairs cluster-matching with Passport ([Gitcoin](https://gitcoin.co/research/quadratic-funding-sybil-resistance)). Freeze eligibility before the round is announced to avoid Beanstalk-style borrowed-power attacks ([Veridise](https://veridise.com/?p=20343)). The ballot should offer only factories that QA has pre-qualified, each shown with its lot-test history, lead time and price, in randomized order. DAO research finds that option position and author-selected options sway vote share ([arXiv 2607.09435](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09435v1)). Hide tallies until the round closes with commit-reveal or shielded voting, so voters cannot bandwagon or prove their vote to a buyer ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/105201/snapshot-adds-shielded-voting-daos-help-solve-voter-apathy)). Ban supplier-funded voter incentives outright. Disclose team voting power up front, since Jupiter's backlash was about team votes ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/358988/dao-behind-dex-aggregator-jupiter-suspends-governance-votes-until-early-2026-amid-community-concerns)). Publish the override rules in advance: the company keeps a veto for safety and compliance, triggered only by named conditions. The lesson from JELLY is that an ad-hoc override reads as centralization, while a pre-written one reads as policy. Votes can run off-chain as signed ballots, because they move no funds. Moderate the discussion so it covers analytical results and logistics only, never which lot "works".

### The voting streak: smaller, decaying, and with no referral component

The current streak gives +2% discount and +0.1x referral per consecutive round, up to +6% and +0.3x after three rounds. **Drop the referral boost entirely.** It turns a governance click into extra USDC commission, which is the cash-for-votes pattern. At the top token level it does nothing anyway: 3.0x plus 0.3x is 33%, above the 30% cap. Change the discount to **+1% per eligible vote, up to +4%**. Missing a round should step it down by 1% rather than resetting it to zero, which removes the last-minute deadline scramble. A counted "abstain / no preference" option lets members keep their streak without adding noise to the result. Only attested purchasers can earn the streak. Record it as a **non-transferable Token-2022 badge**, so the status cannot be bought ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/what-is-token-2022)). A discount is consumptive, a price reduction rather than income, which keeps it on the safe side of both the digital-tool framing and tax treatment. A few further ideas are untested in the sources: requiring a voter to open the lot dossier before the vote counts, or applying the streak discount to lots from rounds the member voted in so that voters share in the outcome they chose. Run either as an experiment, not a promise. No study measures decision quality under participation-only rewards, so ZUMA should track how voting splits against QA outcomes and adjust.

### Commitment without yield: a lock that buys access, not power

Vote-escrow's useful idea is that time committed signals alignment. Its harmful idea is that committed capital earns fees and votes. ZUMA's site already plans a "Priority" tier with a 48-hour head start and members-only drops. Make Priority a **lock of $ZUMA for 30, 90 or 180 days**, with access windows that scale with lock length and decay as the unlock date nears, like veCRV's linear decay ([Curve docs](https://docs.curve.finance/user/vecrv/what-is-vecrv)). Unlocking should take a delay, following Marinade's 30-day unlock and Hyperliquid's 7-day unstaking queue ([Marinade](https://help.marinade.finance/the-structure-of-marinades-dao); [Hyperliquid Docs: Staking](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/staking.md)). The lock earns no yield, no fee share and no extra vote weight, and it does not raise USDC referral rates. Jupiter's 2026 rule requiring 69 staked JUP to trade GUM perps is the precedent for linking stake to product access ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/jupiter-quarterly-report-q3-2026)). Basic member pricing should stay open to any linked wallet holding $ZUMA, priced as a general member discount. That keeps the token a cheap access key, the museum-membership profile the SEC release describes.

### Points season, sybil defense and treasury policy

A single **"Season Zero"** before token genesis can seed ownership the way Hyperliquid did, scaled to a shop. Emit a fixed weekly pool of points. Award them only for delivered, cleared net order value. Give referrers 1 point per 4 points their cleared referred customers earn, matching Hyperliquid's ratio. Add a small capped amount for verifying a vial against its on-chain report. Publish the earning formula, unlike Hyperliquid's opaque criteria. Keep the sybil filters private, as Optimism did when it removed 17,000+ addresses and redistributed their share ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/optimism-sybil)). Cap per-account allocation so the result does not repeat EigenLayer's top-2%-take-90%. Distribute fully unlocked and say in advance that **there will be no Season 2**. Hyperliquid ended points at genesis, and seasonal programs elsewhere ended in cliffs. The SEC release treats loyalty airdrops as a distribution method but flags drops that require a quid pro quo ([GT Law](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/3/sec-clarifies-status-of-crypto-assets-under-federal-securities-laws-signals-potential-exemptive-and-safe-harbor-framework)). Counsel must therefore review whether purchase-earned tokens count as a rebate on the buyer's own consumption (the Fuse logic) or as consideration. For sybil defense, the unit should be the account, not the wallet. A delivered order to a real address is ZUMA's strongest primitive. Layer SAS researcher attestations on top of it, and require ID only where money flows. Civic Pass is a possible ID provider, but it may have been wound down in mid-2025 (secondary source), with existing credentials reportedly still working with SAS ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/projects/civic)). Avoid public sybil bounties. LayerZero's bounty drew 3,000+ reports and 30,000 appeals within hours and was paused after a day ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/295274/layerzero-labs-ceo-announces-pause-of-sybil-bounty-hunter-process-after-influx-of-reports)).

Treasury policy should be short and fixed. Do not sell $ZUMA to raise money, run buybacks, fund burns from sales, pay staking yield or share fees. Publish a genesis allocation table with a small insider share, locked, with unlocks on a fixed monthly date. Keep mint authority and any program upgrade authority under a **Squads multisig** ([Streamflow](https://streamflow.finance/blog/solana-dao-tooling)). Fund rewards as a marketing expense in USDC or credit. Publish a quarterly report on token supply, rewards issued, vote outcomes and lot attestations, but not revenue, so the report does not imply holders have a claim on it. Pump.fun's experience shows the cost of broken commitments: its founders' reversal on presales hurt trust ([Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/pump-fun-draws-backlash-after-confirming-pump-ico/)). Any change to member terms should be announced ahead of time and never applied retroactively.

## Launch in four gated steps, token last

The sequencing follows the main finding: product-market fit first, token second. **Step one, before any token**, is to launch the shop with on-chain SAS lot attestations, verified-customer accounts and the revised referral program. That program pays earned levels in USDC or store credit and needs no token, much as Farcaster and Coinbase One ran utility programs without one. This step also includes counsel review of the token and peptide copy, removal of buyback language, RUO moderation rules for all community channels, and a decision on GLP-1-class compounds, which carry the most FDA risk. **Step two** is Season Zero, run for 8–12 weeks on delivered orders, with the "no Season 2" commitment published at the start. Retention should be measured at 3, 6 and 12 months, the horizon recommended for points programs ([DefiPrime](https://defiprime.com/points-based-token-distribution-programs-web3)). **Step three** is token genesis: distribution through earned points only, the allocation table published, member pricing and the Priority lock switched on, and the multisig in place. **Step four** is an advisory factory vote piloted on one or two compounds, followed by an expansion that depends on whether vote outcomes track QA results. The lab-partner builder program opens once attestations have built a public record. Timing deserves one watchpoint. The SEC release, the stalled CLARITY Act, the GENIUS Act's January 2027 effective date and the unresolved DFAL payout question could all shift before launch, so counsel should re-review the design at step three.

## Conclusion

The research inverts the usual token-design question. Most protocols studied gave their tokens "real utility" by tying them to revenue, through fee shares, buybacks, burns or bribe-able votes. That is the one form of utility a US-facing research-peptide shop cannot offer, and in this category it would also invite the community content that undermines the research-use label. ZUMA's durable advantage is something Hyperliquid built at scale and most crypto commerce projects lacked: a claim anyone can verify, here every lot's test report anchored on-chain. Ownership grows from that verifiable record and from rewarding real purchases, not from rising balances. Each recommended change moves a reward from capital to conduct. Referral levels are earned from real customers, streaks are earned by informed members, and Priority access is earned by commitment, while the token remains a membership key.

Some things remain uncertain. No study shows that a tradable commerce token improves retention more than a plain points program, and no regulator has addressed holdings-linked referral rates directly. ZUMA could therefore launch everything above except the tradable token and lose little, which is worth considering seriously. If the token ships, its credibility will depend on the same thing as Hyperliquid's: a short list of published promises that are visibly kept, and none that involve its price.
