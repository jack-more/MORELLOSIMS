## Which longevity telehealth brands exist and what do they sell and charge?

### Takeaway
Healthspan publishes the clearest pricing: rapamycin from $64/mo including labs and clinician, plus topical rapamycin at $115–120/mo. AgelessRx sells rapamycin, NAD+ and other drugs, but I found its prices only on third-party sites. Hone sells sermorelin at $175/mo. I found no pricing for Maximus, and no Rx menu for Function Health or Superpower.

### Cited Findings
- Healthspan's rapamycin protocol starts at $64/mo. The price covers lab testing, medication and clinician oversight, with no separate consult or membership fee — [Healthspan](https://www.gethealthspan.com/treatments/rapamycin); [Healthspan cost page](https://www.gethealthspan.com/m/rapamycin-cost)
- A third-party review gives Healthspan from about $25/mo, with labs and shipping possibly extra. This conflicts with the $64 figure — [MyPeptideMatch](https://www.mypeptidematch.com/vendors/healthspan)
- Healthspan's own articles disagree on compounded rapamycin cost: one says $60–150/mo, another $20–60/mo — [Healthspan article 1](https://www.gethealthspan.com/research/article/rapamycin-cost-longevity-prescription-guide); [Healthspan article 2](https://www.gethealthspan.com/research/article/rapamycin-cost-longevity-prescription)
- Healthspan sells Topical Rapamycin Skin at $115/mo and Topical Rapamycin+ Hair at $120/mo. TreatCompare also lists Function Health and Superpower but captured no prices for them (checked 2026-10-02) — [TreatCompare](https://www.treatcompare.com/us/telehealth/longevity)
- Hone Health sermorelin is $175/mo; GobyMeds sermorelin is from $199/mo — [TreatCompare](https://www.treatcompare.com/us/telehealth/longevity)
- AgelessRx starts at about $85/mo (third-party); another review gives a $25–1,695/mo range — [MyPeptideMatch](https://www.mypeptidematch.com/vendors/agelessrx); [Nebula](https://nebula.org/blog/agelessrx-review)
- AgelessRx NAD+ injections cost $199–499/mo, with a $99 initial consult and $49–79 follow-ups (Spanish-language comparison site; low reliability) — [FormBlends](https://formblends.com/es/articles/comparison-hub/ageless-rx-nad-review-2026)
- Other telehealth NAD+ programs cost about $119–249/mo — [HealingMaps](https://healingmaps.com/best-nad-therapy-online/)

### Inferences
- The going rate for oral rapamycin as a subscription is about $60–100/mo. Generic sirolimus at the GoodRx rate costs roughly $0.85/tab ($76.72 for 90 tabs, [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/sirolimus)), so drug cost is probably $20–30/mo at typical weekly doses. Gross margin is driven by bundled labs and visits more than by the drug.
- Topical rapamycin, at $115–120/mo, is a higher-margin compounded line that Healthspan has already proven.

### Gaps
- No primary AgelessRx price page was fetched, so its rapamycin, metformin, LDN and acarbose prices are unverified.
- Maximus pricing and menu: the search returned only an unrelated crypto token.
- Hone Health's longevity (non-TRT) menu and prices beyond sermorelin.
- Function Health and Superpower: whether they prescribe at all, or only test, was not confirmed.

## Which items carry the most margin and the most demand?

### Takeaway
Compounded, subscription-priced items have the widest gap between price and cost: custom skin formulas, NAD+, lipotropic/MIC injections and topical rapamycin. Very cheap generics (spironolactone, acarbose, metformin) sold inside a subscription also have high percentage margin, but low absolute dollars. On demand, the strongest signals are custom tretinoin formulas and MIC/B12, judging by how many brands sell them, with NAD+ close behind.

### Cited Findings
- Custom tretinoin subscriptions run about $25–40/mo across Curology, Musely and Nurx, and some copycat services auto-enroll at $60+/mo — [GlowNoFilter](https://glownofilter.com/blog/how-to-get-tretinoin-online/); [TheFlowSpace](https://www.theflowspace.com/lists/how-to-get-tretinoin-online/)
- A generic 45 g tretinoin 0.05% tube costs about $101.78 at retail with a coupon — [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/tretinoin)
- Spironolactone 25 mg x30 averages about $8.57 cash — [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/spironolactone/spironolactone-cost-without-insurance)
- Acarbose 25–100 mg x90 costs $24–34 — [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/precose)
- At least eight telehealth sellers price MIC+B12 at $65–179/mo; one guide says $100–200/mo is typical for home-injection kits — [Ivy Rx](https://www.ivyrx.com/blog/lipotropic-injections-cost), plus the vendor pages in the table above
- NAD+ telehealth programs cost about $119–499/mo — [HealingMaps](https://healingmaps.com/best-nad-therapy-online/); [FormBlends](https://formblends.com/es/articles/comparison-hub/ageless-rx-nad-review-2026)

### Inferences
- Compounded skin formulas are probably the best mix of margin, low regulatory risk and repeat purchase. A pharmacy tube likely lasts 1–2 months (Musely ships a 2-month supply for $35/mo), so drug cost per month is low relative to price.
- MIC/B12 is a crowded, price-competitive category, with introductory prices of $65–99. Its margin per unit is high, but the market is commoditized.
- No source gave 503A pharmacy wholesale costs for compounded creams, NAD+, MIC or glutathione, so margin percentages for compounded items are estimates only.

### Gaps

## Which have regulatory risk in 2026?

### Takeaway
The highest risk items are compounded sterile injectables: glutathione (live 2026 FDA notice and recalls), NAD+ (503A Category 1 only, ineligible for 503B per a January 2026 warning letter), and peptides such as GHK-Cu and BPC-157 (in limbo after the April 2026 list changes). Telehealth advertising of compounded drugs is also under active FDA warning-letter enforcement. FDA-approved generics (tretinoin, spironolactone, sirolimus, acarbose) carry low regulatory risk. NMN moved from risky to lawful supplement in September 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Peptides, April 2026:** On 2026-04-15, FDA announced it would remove 12 peptides from 503A Category 2, including BPC-157, TB-500, MOTs-C, Epitalon, Semax, Melanotan II, KPV and injectable GHK-Cu. Topical GHK-Cu was removed from Category 1. Both removals followed withdrawn nominations. Removal does not make these peptides compoundable, because the interim enforcement policy covers only Category 1. A PCAC meeting on 2026-07-23/24 covered seven peptides, and GHK-Cu review is slated before the end of February 2027 — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-peptides-do-not-compound-list-update-2026/)
- Secondary sources conflict on the July 2026 PCAC outcome: one reports a vote for six of seven peptides (not DSIP). The vote is nonbinding — [FDA peptide summary via search, PeptidesBeat](https://peptidesbeat.com/tracker/fda-timeline/) (low-reliability source; check PCAC minutes)
- **NAD+:** FDA's 503A nominations list (updated 2026-05-14) places NAD in Category 1 "under evaluation" — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/media/94155/download). FDA stopped categorizing substances nominated on or after 2025-01-07, so NAD stays Category 1 under the January 2025 interim policy — [FDA 503A bulks page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/bulk-drug-substances-used-compounding-under-section-503a-fdc-act); [A4PC](https://a4pc.org/news/2025-01/fda-releases-final-interim-guidance-on-bulk-drug-substances-for-both-503as-and-503bs)
- A January 2026 FDA warning letter to GenoGenix LLC said NAD is not eligible for 503B compounding — [PrescribedRx](https://prescribedrx.com/nad-injection/) (secondary; the letter itself was not fetched)
- No approved NAD+ injectable exists; NAD was nominated in 2016 — [Halftime Health](https://www.halftime.health/learning-center/nad-plus-regulatory)
- **Glutathione:** FDA told compounders not to use dietary-supplement-grade glutathione in injectables. At least 30 patients had adverse events consistent with endotoxin exposure (fever, chills, shock, sepsis-like symptoms, some hospitalizations), traced to one Medisca ingredient lot. Four Texas pharmacies recalled product — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-reminds-compounders-not-use-dietary-supplement-grade-glutathione-injectables) (page returned 401 to my fetch; facts are from the search summary and law-firm coverage); [Hart Law](https://hartlaw.com/fda-warning-supplement-grade-glutathione-injections); [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/healthcare-associated-infections/current-situation/index.html). **Date flag:** the search summary dated the FDA notice 2026-08-27, but I could not confirm this on the primary page.
- **Telehealth advertising enforcement:** in September 2025, FDA and HHS began acting against telehealth advertising of compounded drugs — [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/fda-hhs-taking-action-against-telehealths-compounded-drug-advertising). A tracker counts about 80 warning letters and 40 untitled letters in September 2025, 30 warning letters in March 2026, and 25 warning letters the week of 2026-06-15, mostly about GLP-1s — [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/fdas-focus-returns-to-compounding-and-telehealth-another-wave-of-warning-letters); [FormBlends tracker](https://formblends.com/articles/glp1-hub/fda-warning-letters-glp1-telehealth-tracker) (counts are from secondary sources)
- **B12 add-ins:** Eli Lilly reported impurities in all 10 compounded tirzepatide samples it tested, some sold with B12 added — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/compounded-glp-1s-with-b12-pose-safety-risks--lilly-says)
- FDA keeps a general compounding inspections, recalls and actions page — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/compounding-inspections-recalls-and-other-actions)
- **NMN:** FDA letters dated 2025-09-29 said NMN is not excluded from the dietary-supplement definition, reversing the 2022 exclusion. A 2025-12-02 letter to SyncoZymes reinstated its NDI acknowledgment. FDA said this is not a safety determination — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/10/fda-declares-nicotinamide-mononucleotide-is); [NPA](https://www.npanational.org/news/fda-reinstates-nmn-as-dietary-supplement-after-npa-lawsuit/); [CIRS](https://www.cirs-group.com/en/food/us-fda-confirms-nmn-lawful-in-dietary-supplements)
- **Hydroquinone:** in April 2022, FDA sent warning letters to 12 companies selling OTC hydroquinone products (up to 2%). FDA said no OTC skin lightener is legally marketed; Tri-Luma (4% HQ) is the only approved HQ drug. Risks include ochronosis, which can be permanent — [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Health_News/2022/04/20/FDA-skin-lightening-products/9551650480469/); [MedicineNet](https://www.medicinenet.com/fda_warns_companies_about_skin_lightening_products/news.htm). This is a dated (2022) source.
- **Methylene blue:** approved only for methemoglobinemia. I found no specific FDA warning on wellness or supplement use in 2025–2026. The risks are serotonin toxicity alongside SSRIs/SNRIs and G6PD deficiency, and there are no human lifespan trials — [NPR via WFAE](https://www.wfae.org/health/2025-12-15/whats-behind-the-wellness-claims-for-the-synthetic-dye-methylene-blue); [Illuminate Labs](https://illuminatelabs.org/blogs/health/is-methylene-blue-safe)

### Inferences
- **Lowest risk:** approved generics prescribed after a real clinical assessment. These are tretinoin, spironolactone (with potassium and pregnancy counseling), sirolimus off-label (with labs), acarbose and metformin.
- **Moderate risk:** compounded non-sterile topicals (formula creams, HQ blends, topical rapamycin) and oral LDN, provided they are patient-specific 503A preparations and not "essentially copies" of approved drugs.
- **High risk:** sterile injectables (NAD+, glutathione, MIC, methylcobalamin), because of USP <797> sterility, the glutathione endotoxin event, 503B ineligibility for NAD, and FDA's active warning letters on telehealth advertising. Any peptide other than Category 1 items (e.g. GHK-Cu, BPC-157) has no lawful compounding pathway today.
- **Marketing language risk:** claims of "anti-aging", "longevity" or "fat burning", and comparisons to approved drugs, are what the 2025–2026 telehealth warning letters target (inferred from the GLP-1 letter pattern).

### Gaps
- Glutathione's 503A category and the exact date of the FDA glutathione notice (the primary page could not be fetched).
- Methylcobalamin, MIC components, L-carnitine and biotin 503A bulks categories were not checked.
- Regulatory status of NAD+ nasal spray, and any state-board actions on IV NAD+ (California is flagged by DJ Holt Law but not detailed).
- Primary FDA warning letter texts (GenoGenix, telehealth letters) were not opened.
- USP <797> requirements were not researched in this pass.
- Sleep and focus non-controlled products (e.g. compounded melatonin, trazodone off-label, modafinil is controlled) were not researched.
