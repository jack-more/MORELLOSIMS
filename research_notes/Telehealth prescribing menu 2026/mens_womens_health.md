## Q1: Which categories bring in the most revenue for telehealth brands in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
At Hims & Hers, the big disclosed buckets are weight loss (GLP-1), sexual health and dermatology/hair. Together, men's derm, women's derm and sexual health brought in more than $1B in 2025 out of $2.35B total. The Hers brand is about 40% of US revenue. Testosterone, menopause and labs are the new lines that management says can each pass $100M. Midi Health is the largest menopause-focused player, at about a $150M run rate (company claim) and a >$1B valuation. Ro's ED/sexual health was about ⅓ of revenue in 2024 (third-party estimate).

### Cited Findings
- Hims & Hers FY2025 revenue was $2.35B (+59% YoY), with over 2.5M subscribers and Q4 monthly revenue per average subscriber of $83. — [Motley Fool Q4 2025 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/23/hims-hers-hims-earnings-call-transcript/) (AI-assisted transcript; verify against the SEC filing)
- The CFO said men's dermatology, women's dermatology and sexual health together helped the company pass $1B in revenue. Hims-brand revenue grew more than 30% in 2025, and "nearly half a million subscribers" use the "daily solutions" (daily ED). — [Motley Fool Q4 2025 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/23/hims-hers-hims-earnings-call-transcript/)
- The CFO said: "the majority of our revenues are actually coming from outside of the GLP-1 business today." — [Motley Fool Q4 2025 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/23/hims-hers-hims-earnings-call-transcript/)
- CEO Dudum said testosterone support, menopause and labs "can each eclipse $100M in annual revenue in the near future." Menopause, labs and hormone therapies launched in 2025. ### Inferences
- Outside GLP-1s, ED and hair loss are still the cash-pay core. They have the deepest proven demand and the fattest margins (pills cost about $0.35–0.40 wholesale versus subscription pricing). However, they are crowded and ad-cost-heavy.
- Testosterone (TRT plus enclomiphene) and menopause HRT are the fastest-growing "new" categories. A newcomer has a shot at differentiating there before the incumbents scale them past $100M.
- Women's derm (spironolactone, oral minoxidil, tretinoin) is a quiet high-margin driver of Hers' growth.

### Gaps
- No public line-item revenue by category (ED, hair, TRT, menopause) for Hims, Ro or Maximus. Only management qualitative statements and estimates exist.
- Ro's 2025 revenue is unverified. Maximus, Hone, Alloy, Evernow, Winona and Nurx have no revenue disclosures found.
- I did not read the Q2 2026 10-Q MD&A or risk factors (the fetch was truncated at 100k characters).

## Q2: How do DEA telemedicine flexibilities (2026) affect testosterone prescribing without an in-person exam? Any state rules?

### Takeaway
Through **December 31, 2026**, DEA-registered practitioners can prescribe Schedule II–V drugs, including testosterone (Schedule III), by telemedicine without a prior in-person exam, under the DEA/HHS fourth temporary extension. That deadline is ten weeks away. I found no confirmation of a fifth extension or a final special-registration rule, so a new brand building a TRT line faces a real policy cliff.

### Cited Findings
- DEA and HHS issued a fourth temporary extension of the COVID-19 telemedicine flexibilities for prescribing controlled substances, running through Dec 31, 2026. Without it, the flexibilities would have expired Dec 31, 2025. — [Holland & Knight, Jan 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/dea-and-hhs-extend-telemedicine-prescribing-flexibilities-through-2026); [McDermott+](https://www.mcdermottplus.com/insights/dea-extends-telemedicine-flexibilities-for-controlled-substance-prescribing-for-2026/); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/telehealth/dea-extends-telemedicine-prescribing-flexibilities-through-2026/)
- The extension allows prescribing of Schedule II–V controlled substances via telemedicine without a prior in-person evaluation. The stated reason is to give DEA time to finalize permanent rules. — [Gordon Rees](https://www.grsm.com/insight/hhs-and-dea-extend-telemedicine-flexibilities-for-prescribing-controlled-medications-through-2026/); [Virginia Telehealth Network](https://www.ehealthvirginia.org/dea-grants-fourth-temporary-extension-of-flexibilities-for-tele-prescribing-controlled-substances-through-2026/)
- The January 2025 proposed "special registration" rule for telemedicine has not been finalized by the current administration. — [California Telehealth Resource Center](https://caltrc.org/blog/dea-telehealth-rule-extension-understanding-the-2026-landscape/); [McDermott Law](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/dea-telemedicine-flexibilities-for-controlled-substances-2026/)
- TRT monitoring includes two morning testosterone draws ($50–$145) plus periodic hematocrit and PSA. — [policylab.us](https://policylab.us/testosterone-replacement-therapy/online-trt/) (affiliate site)

### Inferences
- Testosterone is a Schedule III anabolic steroid under the Anabolic Steroid Control Act **[background, unverified this session]**. The prescriber needs a DEA registration in each state where patients are located **[background]**.
- If the flexibilities lapse on Jan 1, 2027 without a replacement, the Ryan Haight Act would again require an in-person exam (or a telemedicine exception) before prescribing. TRT brands would then need in-person visits (e.g., lab partner or clinic networks) or a special registration. This is a planning risk for a 2026 launch. Enclomiphene/clomiphene ("non-controlled T support") is the hedge, and that likely explains why Hims and Maximus push it.
- Women's low-dose testosterone is also Schedule III, so the same cliff applies.

### Gaps
- **Most important gap:** whether DEA has issued (or proposed) a fifth extension or final rule between January and October 2026. I found none. Check the Federal Register and DEA's telemedicine page before relying on this.
- State rules were not researched in this session. Known areas to check: state laws requiring an in-person exam or an established relationship before prescribing controlled substances, state laws that additionally schedule hCG or other hormones, and state PDMP check requirements **[background]**. No source fetched.

## Q3: Which items carry meaningful FDA enforcement risk in 2026?

### Takeaway
The highest risk items are anything that is not FDA-approved and whose 503A compounding eligibility is doubtful or contested: **enclomiphene, gonadorelin, kisspeptin, compounded PT-141, compounded hCG (prohibited)**. Compounded BHRT is next (legal under 503A, but FDA says it has no evidence it is safe or effective, plus advertising scrutiny). Beyond any single molecule, FDA is now going directly after **telehealth marketing claims**: 100+ letters in Sept 2025, including Hims, and 30 more to telehealth firms in Feb/Mar 2026. Approved generics (ED, finasteride, estradiol, contraceptives, spironolactone) are low risk.

### Cited Findings
**Enclomiphene**
- FDA advisory committee (PCAC) materials record FDA proposing to **exclude** enclomiphene citrate from the 503A bulks list. These are older (circa 2022) documents. — [FDA PCAC briefing](https://www.fda.gov/media/159042/download); [FDA CDER document](https://www.fda.gov/media/161293/download)
- Enclomiphene is not FDA-approved as a standalone product. Hims sells it at $99/mo (10-month plan), $139/mo (5-month) or $199/mo (3-month) plus a $94 lab kit. — [Hims enclomiphene price guide](https://www.hims.com/blog/enclomiphene-price) (page dated about Jan 2026). Maximus about $99.99/mo on a 12-month plan, labs separate. — [crowncounseling.com](https://crowncounseling.com/reviews/best-online-trt) (affiliate)
- Compounding-pharmacy price for enclomiphene 25 mg daily is about $30–90/month. — [policylab.us](https://policylab.us/testosterone-replacement-therapy/online-trt/); [compoundingfinder.com](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/blog/enclomiphene-cost-guide) (commercial)
- OPSS (DoD) says clomiphene and enclomiphene are drugs, not dietary supplements. — [OPSS](https://www.opss.org/article/clomiphene-and-enclomiphene-drugs-not-dietary-supplements)
- I found no FDA warning letter naming enclomiphene in 2024–2026. The FDA compounding enforcement page lists many 2025–2026 503A/503B letters, but the drugs were not checked one by one. — [FDA compounding actions](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/compounding-inspections-recalls-and-other-actions)

**hCG / gonadorelin / kisspeptin / PT-141**
- hCG has been regulated as a biologic since March 23, 2020, which ended pharmacy compounding. — [PCCA](https://pccarx.com/Blog/compounding-update-hcg). 503A and 503B pharmacies cannot make biologics, so pharmacies still offering compounded hCG are outside the rules. — [Compounding Finder](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/medications/hcg/safety)
- Brand Pregnyl and Novarel are described as intermittently available, with recombinant Ovidrel the most stable (commercial source; unverified). — [Remy Peptides](https://remypeptides.com/research/hcg-gonadotropin-research-reference-protocol). One telehealth firm offers only Pregnyl, Novarel and Ovidrel. — [Superpower](https://superpower.com/guides/hcg)
- Gonadorelin: no marketed FDA-approved product (Factrel and LutrePulse discontinued). — [Superpower gonadorelin guide](https://superpower.com/guides/peptides-for-testosterone). Its compounding category conflicts: Category 1 per [PeptideLaws](https://peptidelaws.com/news/fda-peptide-category-1-vs-category-2-list-compounding-access), "compoundable" per [OneTwenty](https://onetwenty.com/blog/testosterone-peptides).
- Kisspeptin-10 is listed as "Still Category 2" by [PrescribedRx](https://prescribedrx.com/peptides/) and as not eligible for compounding by [OneTwenty](https://onetwenty.com/blog/testosterone-peptides).
- PT-141: Category 2 (not compoundable) per [PeptideLaws](https://peptidelaws.com/news/fda-peptide-category-1-vs-category-2-list-compounding-access); compoundable per [PrescribedRx](https://prescribedrx.com/peptides/). **These sources conflict.**
- Some vendor sources report that FDA removed several peptides from Category 2 in April 2026 after nominators withdrew them, and that PCAC voted July 23–24, 2026 to recommend some peptides for the 503A list. These reports are **not confirmed by an FDA primary source**, and it is unclear which peptides they cover. — [OneTwenty](https://onetwenty.com/blog/peptide-companies); [Superpower](https://superpower.com/guides/peptides-for-testosterone); [peptideverdict](https://www.peptideverdict.com/blog/fda-peptide-compounding-timeline)

**Menopausal hormones**
- On Nov 10, 2025, FDA announced it would remove boxed warnings on menopausal hormone therapy. On Feb 12, 2026 it approved the first six relabeled products: Prometrium, Divigel, Cenestin, Enjuvia, Estring and Bijuva. 29 manufacturers have submitted updates. The removed statements cover CVD, breast cancer and probable dementia. The endometrial cancer boxed warning remains on systemic estrogen-only products. — [Drug Topics](https://www.drugtopics.com/view/fda-removes-black-box-warning-on-6-menopausal-hormone-therapy-products); [Contemporary OB/GYN](https://www.contemporaryobgyn.net/view/fda-initiates-removal-of-boxed-warnings-from-hormone-therapy-for-menopause); [Urology Times](https://www.urologytimes.com/view/fda-approves-label-changes-for-first-batch-of-menopausal-hormone-therapy-products); [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2841321)
- FDA says it has no evidence that compounded "bioidentical hormones" are safe and effective, or safer or more effective than FDA-approved therapy. There are no FDA-approved estriol drugs. — [FDA Menopause page](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/womens-health-topics/menopause) (page may be dated)
- The 2020 NASEM report found insufficient data that compounded BHRT is as safe as or safer than approved therapy (secondary citation). — [thehrtindex / search summary](https://thehrtindex.com/fda-approved-bioidentical-hormones/). The label changes apply only to approved products, not to compounded ones.

**Telehealth advertising enforcement**
- On Sept 16, 2025, FDA released 100+ warning and untitled letters, including a "cease and desist"-style warning letter to Hims & Hers (dated Sept 9, 2025) over claims that its compounded products contained "the same active ingredient" as Ozempic/Wegovy. — [FDA warning letter to Hims](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/hims-hers-health-inc-dba-hims-716567-09092025); [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/health-news/drug-center/fda-cracks-down-on-hims-other-telehealth-companies-over-drug-ads)
- A follow-up batch of 30 letters (dated Feb 20, 2026, released Mar 3, 2026) targeted telehealth firms over compounded GLP-1 sameness claims and over not making clear the telehealth firm is not the compounder. — [telecareaware](https://telecareaware.com/tag/hhs/); [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-warning-letters-hims-novo-nordisk-compounded-glp1/)
- Hims pulled "the Pill" (its compounded oral GLP-1 launch) from market to "prioritize engagement with ecosystem stakeholders." The CEO declined to discuss ongoing FDA/DOJ matters. — [Motley Fool Q4 2025 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/23/hims-hers-hims-earnings-call-transcript/)

### Inferences
- Risk tiers for a new brand:
  - **Avoid or legal review first:** kisspeptin, compounded PT-141, compounded hCG, gonadorelin.
  - **Elevated:** enclomiphene (FDA's proposed exclusion from the bulks list, and no approved product; widely sold anyway), compounded BHRT/estriol, women's testosterone (Schedule III, off-label).
  - **Moderate:** compounded ED troches and topical fin/min (legal 503A, but marketing claims are scrutinized).
  - **Low:** approved generics.
- Marketing copy is now the enforcement vector. Do not imply compounded products are "the same as" or "generic" approved drugs, and disclose who compounds them.
- Background, unverified this session: there is no FDA-approved PE drug in the US. Vyleesi (bremelanotide) is approved for premenopausal HSDD. Flibanserin (Addyi) is an alternative. There is no FDA-approved testosterone product for women. Opill is OTC (2024). Levonorgestrel EC is OTC; ulipristal (ella) is Rx.

### Gaps
- The current official 503A bulks-list category for enclomiphene, gonadorelin, PT-141 and kisspeptin as of Oct 2026 was not confirmed from FDA's own list pages. Sources conflict, and an April 2026 change and July 2026 PCAC vote are reported only by vendors.
- No 2025–2026 FDA statement specific to compounded BHRT was found, beyond the standing FDA page.
- Not verified from first-party pages: consumer prices for Hims and Ro ED and hair products (hims.com returned 403), plus Maximus, Alloy, Ro's TRT and Midi cash prices.
- Wholesale (NADAC/acquisition) costs for testosterone cypionate, anastrozole, clomiphene, estradiol, spironolactone, tretinoin and compounded APIs were not collected. Margins in the table are **inferred**, not sourced.
