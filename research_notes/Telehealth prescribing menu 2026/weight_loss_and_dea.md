Research date: 2026-10-20. Confidence key: [P] = primary source (FDA, DEA, SEC, court, manufacturer); [L] = law-firm or trade press; [A] = affiliate, price-comparison or vendor site (low confidence; used only where nothing better was found).

## 1. Branded GLP-1 cash-pay channels (LillyDirect, NovoCare, oral Wegovy, Foundayo/orforglipron, TrumpRx). Can a telehealth brand route patients there, and where does its revenue come from?

### Takeaway
All four branded cash-pay products are live and priced at $149–$449/month at manufacturer-set prices: Zepbound vials, Wegovy pen, Wegovy pill (approved Dec 22, 2025) and Foundayo/orforglipron (approved Apr 1, 2026). TrumpRx is a referral layer that points to these same programs, not a separate supply. A telehealth brand can route its patients into these programs, as Ro, Teladoc, LifeMD, Noom, WeightWatchers, GoodRx and now Hims do. However, I found no evidence that the brand earns any spread on the drug, which sells at the manufacturer's price. Its revenue is the membership or consult fee, for example Ro at about $149/month on top of the drug.

### Inferences
- A new brand can legally offer all four branded products. The routes are e-prescribing to NovoCare Pharmacy or LillyDirect's pharmacy partners (Gifthealth and others), with the patient paying the manufacturer price, or a formal integration agreement with those pharmacies. Because the manufacturer sets the price and the pharmacy collects it, the brand's revenue on this line is the membership or consult fee only. The Ro model (drug at LillyDirect parity plus a $149/month membership) is the benchmark.
- Hims's gross margin falling from 76% to 64% while it moved to branded GLP-1s is consistent with branded drugs carrying little or no margin for the platform. This is an inference; Hims does not break out weight-loss margin.
- The lowest-friction, lowest-cost entry products for patients are the Wegovy pill ($149 start, $299 maintenance) and Foundayo ($149 start, $299 maintenance). These pills avoid needles and cold-chain logistics. Zepbound vials at $449 are the efficacy-premium option.
- Risk level for branded routing: LOW legally. The commercial risk is dependence on manufacturer programs that can be changed or cancelled at any time, and on promotional dates expiring Dec 31, 2026.

### Gaps
- I found no source showing that any telehealth company earns a drug-level spread, rebate or referral fee from LillyDirect, NovoCare or TrumpRx. The commercial terms of the Gifthealth, Ro and Teladoc integration agreements are not public.
- I could not fetch TrumpRx.gov directly; current TrumpRx listings come from affiliate sites only.
- No official Hims price for branded Wegovy, Zepbound or Foundayo in 2026, and no weight-loss subscriber count or revenue split.
- Ro's 2026 membership price comes from third-party reviews, not Ro's own page.
- The $249 Wegovy subscription tier is unverified.

## 2. Compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide: FDA status, "essentially a copy," the 503B bulks proposal, warning letters, OFA v. FDA, "personalized" compounding, risk

### Takeaway
The legal pathway for mass-market compounded GLP-1s has nearly closed. Both shortages are over, and courts have upheld FDA, including the Fifth Circuit on Aug 27, 2026. FDA's Apr 1, 2026 policy treats additive combos such as semaglutide + B12 as "essentially a copy" and tolerates only about 4 or fewer prescriptions per month per product for a 503A pharmacy, unless a genuine patient-specific "significant difference" is documented. FDA proposed on Apr 30, 2026 to keep semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide off the 503B bulks list. It sent 30 warning letters to telehealth firms in Feb 2026, 25 more on Jun 16, 2026, and an "essentially a copy" letter to Empower Pharmacy in Sept 2026 that attacks templated "personalization." Risk for a new brand built on compounded GLP-1s: HIGH.

### Cited Findings
**Shortage end and court rulings**
- Tirzepatide was taken off the shortage list in 2024 and semaglutide in 2025. Neither is on the shortage list or the 503B bulks list — [Polsinelli / JDSupra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/fda-signals-it-has-no-appetite-to-add-8192436/) [L]; [Foley, Apr 2026](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/04/fda-clarifies-policies-for-pharmacy-compounders-of-glp-1-products/) [L]; [McDermott, semaglutide shortage resolved](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/semaglutide-shortage-resolved/) [L]
- OFA v. FDA (tirzepatide): the N.D. Texas court ruled for FDA and Lilly (PI denied Mar 5, 2025; merits ruling May 7, 2025, per different sources). On Aug 27, 2026 a Fifth Circuit panel (Richman, Duncan, Oldham; per curiam, No. 25-10600) affirmed. It rejected the arguments that notice-and-comment was required and that the decision was arbitrary and capricious — [Fifth Circuit opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/unpub/25/25-10600.0.pdf) [P]; [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/pharmacy/court-sides-with-fda-in-tirzepatide-shortage-fight-5-notes/) [L]; [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/court-backs-fda-in-tirzepatide-compounding-case/) [L]
- Conflict on the semaglutide companion appeal: Becker's says it was still pending, with a separate opinion to follow, while Clark Hill says the Fifth Circuit upheld both the semaglutide and tirzepatide delistings — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/pharmacy/court-sides-with-fda-in-tirzepatide-shortage-fight-5-notes/) [L]; [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/fifth-circuit-upholds-fda-glp1-shortage-decisions/) [L]

**503A "essentially a copy" policy (FDA statement Apr 1, 2026)**
- A compounded GLP-1 is essentially a copy if it has the same API as a commercially available product in the same, similar or easily substitutable strength, and that product can be used by the same route, "regardless of its labeling." The only exception is where "the prescriber determines and documents that the compounded drug product contains a change that produces a significant difference for an identified individual patient" — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/04/fda-clarifies-policies-for-pharmacy-compounders-of-glp-1-products/) [L]
- Semaglutide + B12 (cyanocobalamin) is essentially a copy when given by the same route and each ingredient is within 10% of its marketed strength — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/04/fda-clarifies-policies-for-pharmacy-compounders-of-glp-1-products/) [L]; [Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-moves-to-permanently-close-the-door-on-compounded-glp-1s) [L]
- Enforcement discretion: FDA does not intend, "at this time," to act against a 503A that compounds an essentially-copy product for "four or fewer prescriptions for that compounded drug product during a calendar month." The statement "does not have the force and effect of law" — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/04/fda-clarifies-policies-for-pharmacy-compounders-of-glp-1-products/) [L]
- Empower Pharmacy warning letter (Sept 2026): FDA called semaglutide/cyanocobalamin and tirzepatide/niacinamide products essentially copies. It said the production volume suggested the differences were "pretextual," and it flagged templated significant-difference language and prescriptions with no determination at all. FDA also said that technology platforms giving prescribers pre-selected menu options for significant-difference statements call those determinations into question. Two federal suits challenging this were filed, and both preliminary injunctions were denied — [Lumalex Law](https://www.lumalexlaw.com/2026/09/24/empower-the-b-vitamin-and-the-word-pretextual/) [L]; [Buchanan Ingersoll](https://www.bipc.com/fda-warning-letter-to-empower-pharmacy-and-what-it-means-for-503a-compounding-pharmacies) [L]; [ScriptLinkRx](https://scriptlinkrx.com/blog/fda-warning-letter-empower-compounded-glp-1) [A]

**503B bulks list proposal**
- FDA proposed on Apr 30, 2026 to exclude semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the 503B bulks list. It was published in the Federal Register May 1, 2026, with comments due Jun 29, 2026. FDA found no clinical need to compound from bulk absent a shortage. Makary: "When FDA-approved drugs are available, outsourcing facilities cannot lawfully compound using bulk drug substances unless there is a clear clinical need" — [JDSupra / Epstein Becker Green](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/fda-proposal-would-leave-semaglutide-7717865/) [L]; [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-glp1-503b-bulks-list-proposed-exclusion-semaglutide-tirzepatide-liraglutide/) [L]; [Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/fda-signals-it-has-no-appetite-to-add-popular-glp-1) [L]
- Liraglutide remains on the shortage list, so 503Bs can still compound it for now despite the proposal — [JDSupra / EBG](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/fda-proposal-would-leave-semaglutide-7717865/) [L]
- I found no source showing the proposal had been finalized as of Oct 2026.

**Warning letters to telehealth companies**
- Sept 2025: about 100 cease-and-desist letters on deceptive DTC advertising of compounded drugs — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-warning-letters-hims-novo-nordisk-compounded-glp1/) [L]. Another tracker counts about 80 warning letters and 40 untitled letters, or more than 85 warning letters from Sept 2025 to Mar 2026; counts differ by source — [telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/fda-sends-new-round-of-warning-letters-over-compounded-glp-1-marketing-claims/) [L]; [FormBlends tracker](https://formblends.com/articles/glp1-hub/fda-warning-letters-glp1-telehealth-tracker) [A]
- Feb 20, 2026 (public Mar 3): 30 warning letters to telehealth and wellness firms under FDCA 502(a) and 502(bb). They cited claims of "the same ingredients" as branded drugs, "tested in clinical trials," and obscuring the compounded nature or source. Firms had 15 working days to respond, with threatened seizure and injunction — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-warning-letters-hims-novo-nordisk-compounded-glp1/) [L]; [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/03/glp1-compliance-fda-targets-telehealth-marketing-in-30-new-warning-letters/) [L]; [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-ramps-crackdown-glp-1-drug-compounders-fresh-batch-30-warning-letters) [L]
- Jun 16, 2026: 25 warning letters to telehealth companies over compounded semaglutide, tirzepatide and, in one case, liraglutide. The violations were (1) website product images with the telehealth company's own name on the label, implying it was the compounder; (2) claims that products were FDA-approved or evaluated; and (3) claims that the pharmacy or outsourcing facility was "FDA-approved" or "FDA-licensed." The letters cite misbranding under 502(a) and 502(bb) and a prohibited act under 301(a). Firms must identify the compounders and provide labeling within 15 business days — [McDermott, Jun 30, 2026](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/fda-takes-aim-at-misleading-online-claims-for-compounded-glp-1-drugs/) [L]; [Men's Journal citing Reuters](https://www.mensjournal.com/news/fda-warning-telehealth-compounded-weight-loss-drugs) [L]
- Date conflict: some secondary sources give Jun 8, 2026 for the 25-letter batch; McDermott gives Jun 16 — [telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/fda-sends-new-round-of-warning-letters-over-compounded-glp-1-marketing-claims/) [L]
- Early 2026: Hims announced, then abandoned, a compounded version of oral semaglutide under legal pressure. Makary pledged "swift action" against "illegal copycat drugs" — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-warning-letters-hims-novo-nordisk-compounded-glp1/) [L]

**Market pricing for compounded GLP-1s (retail)**
- A 2026 index puts the median compounded semaglutide price at $177/month and tirzepatide at $240/month. The cheapest 10% of providers charge about $99 and $145 — [WeightLossRankings GLP-1 pricing index](https://www.weightlossrankings.org/research/glp1-pricing-index) [A]
- Vendor-quoted compounded tirzepatide is $299 (2.5–5 mg), $349 (7.5–10 mg) and $399 (12.5–15 mg), and other vendors quote $349–$699 — [FormBlends](https://formblends.com/articles/answers-hub/compounded-tirzepatide-cost-complete-2026-pricing-guide) [A]; [healthymealsincentives](https://healthymealsincentives.org/tirzepatide-cost/) [A]
- A provider-by-provider price database (June 2026) exists but its method is unverified — [glp1costguide](https://glp1costguide.com/price-database/) [A]

### Inferences
- Branded cash prices ($149–$299 for the pills, $349 for the Wegovy pen, $449 for the Zepbound vial) now overlap with compounded retail prices ($177–$240 median). That removes most of compounding's price advantage, which is the strategic logic behind Hims's pivot.
- What providers still do, and the risk attached:
  - (a) Additive combos (B12, glycine, niacinamide, pyridoxine) are explicitly treated as essentially copies when strengths are within 10%. HIGH risk.
  - (b) Off-label dose changes or titration strengths with a templated "significant difference" note are attacked by name in the Empower letter, including tech-platform dropdowns. HIGH risk.
  - (c) Low-volume compounding of 4 or fewer prescriptions per product per month per pharmacy is within FDA's stated enforcement discretion, but that cannot support a DTC business.
  - (d) A genuine patient-specific need documented by the prescriber (for example, a documented excipient allergy) is lawful but is a niche.
- Marketing rules for any brand that still offers compounded drugs: never put your own brand name on the vial image; never say "same ingredient as Wegovy/Zepbound," "clinically proven," or "FDA-approved/registered pharmacy"; and disclose the compounding pharmacy by name.
- Overall risk for a new brand launching compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide as a core product: HIGH, for regulatory, litigation (Novo and Lilly have sued telehealth firms), payment-processor and advertising-platform reasons.

### Gaps
- No reliable source for compounded GLP-1 acquisition cost (503A per-vial wholesale or API cost). Affiliate pages give only illustrative figures, so no defensible compounded margin can be stated.
- The status of the semaglutide companion appeal in OFA v. FDA is contested (decided vs. pending).
- Whether the 503B exclusion proposal has been finalized after the Jun 29, 2026 comment close.
- I did not retrieve the FDA warning-letter database entries or the FDA "concerns with unapproved GLP-1 drugs" page directly. Dates and counts come from law-firm summaries.

## 3. Retatrutide and cagrilintide (CagriSema)

### Takeaway
Neither can be lawfully prescribed outside a trial or compounded in Oct 2026. Retatrutide is investigational, with a Lilly filing expected around Q1 2027. CagriSema was filed with FDA on Dec 18, 2025, with a decision expected around Q4 2026, but is not approved as of the latest sources. Risk of offering either: EXTREME, because it means selling an unapproved new drug.

### Cited Findings
- Retatrutide is not FDA-approved and is described as investigational in an August 2026 preprint. Most users who obtained it outside trials got it through online or telehealth vendors, and contamination has been documented — [Preprints.org, Aug 2026](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202608.1193) [L, not peer-reviewed]
- No US pharmacy can legally compound retatrutide; legitimate access is through trials or narrow expanded access. Lilly's filing is reportedly planned for Q1 2027 — [The RX Index, verified Oct 14, 2026](https://therxindex.com/guides/compounded-retatrutide/) [A]
- Novo filed the CagriSema NDA (cagrilintide 2.4 mg + semaglutide 2.4 mg) on Dec 18, 2025, and expects a US decision in Q4 2026. It was not approved as of the latest pages found — [PharmaLive](https://www.pharmalive.com/novo-nordisk-files-for-fda-approval-of-cagrisema/) [L]; [Drugs.com history](https://drugs.com/history/cagrisema.html) [L]; [The RX Index](https://therxindex.com/guides/cagrisema-fda-approval-status/) [A]
- Cagrilintide is not FDA-approved and not on the shortage list, so it cannot be compounded under 503A or 503B — [Doctronic](https://www.doctronic.ai/blog/generic-vs-brand-cagrilintide/) [A]

### Inferences
- If CagriSema is approved in late 2026, it would become a branded cash-pay product, likely through NovoCare, and be routable in the same way as Wegovy. Plan for it as a 2027 item.
- Any "research peptide" retatrutide or cagrilintide offering by a telehealth brand would be a misbranded or unapproved drug and an enforcement and processor-shutdown magnet.

### Gaps
- I found no primary FDA statement on retatrutide or cagrilintide compounding; the legality conclusion rests on statute (503A/503B require approved components or bulks-list or USP substances) as relayed by secondary sources.

## 5. DEA telemedicine rules in 2026, and prescribing Schedule III–V drugs (testosterone CIII, phentermine CIV) without an in-person visit

### Takeaway
DEA and HHS issued a fourth temporary extension of the COVID-era flexibilities, running through Dec 31, 2026. Until then, DEA-registered practitioners can prescribe Schedule II–V drugs by audio-video telemedicine with no prior in-person evaluation (audio-only for Schedule III–V OUD drugs). So in October 2026, testosterone and phentermine can be prescribed via video federally, subject to state law. The permanent special-registration final rule (RIN 1117-AB40) was under OIRA review, targeting November 2026 with text not public, and no fifth extension had been published as of early October. Planning for 2027 is therefore uncertain: risk is HIGH that video-only CIII–CV prescribing will require a special registration or an in-person visit from Jan 1, 2027.

### Cited Findings
- Fourth temporary extension: flexibilities run through Dec 31, 2026 (they would otherwise have expired at the end of 2025). They cover Schedule II–V by audio-video without a prior in-person evaluation, and Schedule III–V OUD narcotics by audio-only. The goal is to avoid a "telemedicine cliff" until final regulations — [Holland & Knight, Jan 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/dea-and-hhs-extend-telemedicine-prescribing-flexibilities-through-2026) [L]; [McDermott+](https://www.mcdermottplus.com/insights/dea-extends-telemedicine-flexibilities-for-controlled-substance-prescribing-for-2026/) [L]; [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/telehealth/dea-extends-telemedicine-prescribing-flexibilities-through-2026/) [L]; [CalTRC](https://caltrc.org/blog/dea-telehealth-rule-extension-understanding-the-2026-landscape/) [L]
- Special registration: the final rule (RIN 1117-AB40) is at final rule stage with a Unified Agenda target of November 2026, and its text is not public. It reached OIRA on Aug 25, 2026 per most sources (one says Sep 21). A Federal Register search on Oct 6, 2026 found no fifth extension and no final rule. The proposed rule (Jan 2025) set an $888 fee per special registration type — [DeKo Law](https://www.dekolaw.com/post/dea-s-telemedicine-special-registration-rule-just-moved-to-final-rule-stage-target-date-november-2) [L]; [telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/dea-telemedicine-special-registration-rule-enters-final-review/) [L]; [WCHSB, Oct 8, 2026](https://insights.wchsb.com/2026/10/08/under-90-days-telehealth-prescribing-rules-are-about-to-change-but-the-final-rule-is-still-under-review/) [L]; [NASCSA](https://nascsa.org/dea-gets-closer-to-finalizing-telemedicine-special-registration/) [L]; [ASPR](https://asprcmsolutions.com/insights/dea-telemedicine-special-registration-2026/) [L]
- January 2025 final rules ("Expansion of Buprenorphine Treatment via Telemedicine Encounter" and "Continuity of Care for VA Patients," both dated Jan 17, 2025) were originally effective Feb 18, 2025. They were delayed to Mar 21, 2025 and then to Dec 31, 2025 — [AHA](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2025-02-14-dea-and-hhs-delay-implementation-buprenorphine-final-rule) [L]; [ACOFP](https://www.acofp.org/news-and-publications/news/full-news-article/2025/03/21/hhs-dea-delay-effective-date-buprenorphine-telemedicine-prescribing-rule-until-2026) [L]; [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/dea-telemedicine-rules-further-delayed-until-nearly-2026) [L]
- The buprenorphine rule allows a 6-month telemedicine-only window before an in-person evaluation is required. Buprenorphine prescriptions may continue under the fourth extension through Dec 31, 2026 — [WCHSB](https://insights.wchsb.com/2026/10/08/under-90-days-telehealth-prescribing-rules-are-about-to-change-but-the-final-rule-is-still-under-review/) [L]; [Univ. of Kentucky p2p](https://p2p.uky.edu/telemedicine) [L]
- One source says the administration had not moved forward with finalizing special registration, while others say the agencies intend to finalize before the deadline. The sources conflict — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/dea-telemedicine-flexibilities-for-controlled-substances-2026/) [L]; [GRSM](https://www.grsm.com/insight/hhs-and-dea-extend-telemedicine-flexibilities-for-prescribing-controlled-medications-through-2026/) [L]
- Phentermine is Schedule IV, and prescribing it requires a DEA registration — noted in multiple sources, including [Qsymia.com (CIV labeling)](https://qsymia.com/) [P]

### Inferences
- **Now through Dec 31, 2026:** testosterone (CIII), phentermine and Qsymia (CIV) can be prescribed via synchronous audio-video telemedicine without an in-person visit, federally. The prescriber needs a DEA registration in the patient's state, plus state licensure and compliance with state rules, which can be stricter.
- **Jan 1, 2027 onward, three scenarios:**
  - (a) A special-registration final rule takes effect, likely with an $888-per-type fee, state-registration requirements, possible PDMP checks and possible CII limits.
  - (b) A fifth extension is issued, which has happened every prior year in November or December.
  - (c) The cliff: the Ryan Haight Act default requires an in-person evaluation (or a covered exception) before prescribing controlled substances.
- A brand launching phentermine or testosterone in Q4 2026 should design its flow so an in-person or partner-clinic visit can be added if needed, and should avoid making controlled substances the anchor product until the 2027 rule is known.
- GLP-1s, Contrave and metformin are not controlled, so the DEA rules do not gate them. Only phentermine, Qsymia and testosterone are affected within this menu.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the Federal Register text of the fourth extension or its FR citation and publication date (law-firm alerts date it to roughly Dec 2025 or Jan 2026).
- The content of the special-registration final rule is unknown (not public as of Oct 2026).
- No verified state-level in-person requirements for controlled substances via telehealth (for example Ohio or Texas anorectic rules). This needs state-board source review.
- I found no primary notice confirming the exact 2026 status of the delayed buprenorphine and VA final rules' effective dates.

## 6. Margins: consult and membership fees and medication markups in compounded and branded GLP-1 telehealth (2026)

### Takeaway
On branded GLP-1s the telehealth brand's margin is essentially the membership fee, for example Ro at $149/month (or $74–$99/month prepaid) on top of pass-through manufacturer pricing. Compounded GLP-1 programs historically bundled drug and care at $99–$400+/month with a large undisclosed drug markup, but no reliable acquisition-cost data was found. Hims's company-wide gross margin fell from 76% to 64% as it shifted to branded drugs, which is the best public signal of how much thinner branded economics are.

### Cited Findings
- Ro's membership is $39 for the first month, then $149/month, or $888/year prepaid; other reviews quote $74–$99/month on 3-, 6- or 12-month prepay. The drug is priced at LillyDirect parity — [The RX Index](https://therxindex.com/guides/ro-vs-lilly-direct/) [A]; [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/ro-review) [L]; [FormBlends, Ro Zepbound cost](https://formblends.com/articles/cost-access/how-much-is-zepbound-through-ro) [A]
- NovoCare notes that telehealth platforms add their own fees on top of the self-pay drug price — [The RX Index](https://therxindex.com/guides/wegovy-pill-cost-without-insurance/) [A]
- Hims gross margin was 64% in Q2 2026 vs 76% in Q2 2025, and cost of revenue rose to $272.4M from $128.6M. FY26 adjusted EBITDA margin guidance is 9–10% — [Hims 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001773751/000177375126000161/hims-20260630x8xkearningsr.htm) [P]
- Compounded retail medians are about $177/month for semaglutide and $240/month for tirzepatide, with the lowest decile at $99/$145. Advertised prices often exclude membership, lab, shipping and dose-increase fees — [WeightLossRankings](https://www.weightlossrankings.org/research/glp1-pricing-index) [A]
- Non-GLP: generic phentermine costs about $11–$32/month at retail, so consult fees dominate the economics — [GoodRx](https://goodrx.com/phentermine) [L]; [Ro](https://ro.co/weight-loss/phentermine-cost/) [L]

### Inferences
- **Branded line unit economics (illustrative, not sourced as a fact):** with a membership of $99–$149/month and a drug pass-through of $149–$449, the platform's gross profit is roughly the membership minus clinician time, pharmacy coordination and support. Customer acquisition cost recovery depends on retention.
- **Compounded line:** historical retail pricing of $177–$299 implies wide drug markups, but these cannot be quantified without wholesale data. The 2026 enforcement posture makes those markups high-risk revenue.
- **Demand signals:** Hims grew subscribers 19% to 2.89M and revenue 38% after pivoting to branded weight loss, and raised FY26 guidance to $3.1–3.3B. That indicates consumer demand persisted through the shift to branded drugs and supports a branded-first menu.
