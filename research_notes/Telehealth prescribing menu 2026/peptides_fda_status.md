# US FDA compounding status of research peptides (503A), as of October 20, 2026

Scope note: US only. The primary FDA documents below were downloaded directly from fda.gov on 2026-10-20. FDA's 503A categories list PDF reads "Updated May 14, 2026", and the 503A bulks web page reads "Content current as of 05/14/2026". This means **FDA has not changed the 503A category lists since May 14, 2026**, despite the July PCAC votes and the October OMB submission. Vendor and peptide-seller blogs were used only to locate primary sources. Where one is cited, it is marked LOW-CONFIDENCE.

Legal frame: a 503A pharmacy may compound from a bulk drug substance only if (1) the substance complies with a USP/NF monograph, if one exists; (2) it is a component of an FDA-approved drug, if there is no monograph; or (3) it appears on the 503A bulks list (21 CFR 216.23). Under FDA's interim policy, FDA does not intend to take action for compounding with Category 1 ("under evaluation") substances when the guidance conditions are met. Category 2 ("significant safety risks") and Category 3 ("nominated without adequate support") substances, and substances on no list at all, are outside that policy. FDA says it "would consider taking action" against those under general enforcement policies. FDA also says it will not place substances nominated on or after January 7, 2025 into any category. — [FDA 503A bulks page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/bulk-drug-substances-used-compounding-under-section-503a-fdc-act)

## Per-peptide status table (objective deliverable)

### Takeaway
As of October 20, 2026, **none of the "research peptides" (BPC-157, TB-500, KPV, MOTS-c, semax, epitalon, DSIP, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, AOD-9604, selank, thymosin alpha-1, LL-37, melanotan II, kisspeptin-10, injectable GHK-Cu) has a lawful 503A route.** None is on the 503A bulks list or in Category 1, and none has a USP monograph or is a component of an approved drug.

The defensible 503A options on this list fall into two groups:
- **Category 1 (interim enforcement discretion):** topical/non-injectable GHK-Cu, NAD+, and glutathione.
- **Monograph or approved-drug-component status:** tesamorelin (Egrifta), bremelanotide (Vyleesi) and oxytocin (Pitocin/USP), plus sermorelin and gonadorelin with weaker footing.

An "Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides" has been at OMB since Oct 14, 2026 and is not yet issued. It could change the picture for BPC-157 and the other PCAC-recommended peptides at short notice.

### Inferences
- **Tesamorelin (Egrifta), bremelanotide (Vyleesi) and oxytocin (Pitocin):** their approved-drug status comes from background knowledge and was not re-verified on Drugs@FDA this session. Because they are FDA-approved, their APIs qualify under the "component of an FDA-approved drug" or USP-monograph routes. 503A still bars regularly compounding "essentially copies" of commercially available drugs, so a compounded version needs a documented patient-specific clinical difference (e.g., a different route or strength).
- **Cash-pay telehealth menu:** the lowest-risk peptide offerings today are (a) brand tesamorelin and bremelanotide; (b) compounded oxytocin, sermorelin and gonadorelin from pharmacies that document their API basis; and (c) Category 1 items (topical GHK-Cu, NAD+, glutathione). For glutathione, require proof of pharmaceutical-grade, injection-suitable API.
- **Category 1 is interim and revocable.** FDA removed and restored topical GHK-Cu within three weeks (Apr 22 → May 14, 2026), so any Category 1 item can disappear quickly.
- **Most likely candidates for interim access:** the six PCAC-recommended peptides (BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, semax, epitalon). DSIP failed. CJC-1295, ipamorelin, AOD-9604 and thymosin alpha-1 drew lopsided 2024 "no" votes from the prior PCAC membership and are not on any current PCAC docket.
- **KPV caveat:** KPV was nominated and reviewed only as a 0.1% topical cream/gel. Any eventual listing or interim access may be limited to that use and form. Injectable or oral KPV would likely remain outside it.
- **Route caveats for other recommended peptides:**
  - BPC-157's FDA review centered on injection.
  - Semax was nominated for intranasal and SC.
  - Epitalon, MOTS-c and TB-500 were nominated for injection only.
  - Oral or nasal versions of these may not be covered even if they are listed.

### Gaps
- **Pre-April 2026 Category 1 status of GHK-Cu (non-injectable):** not confirmed from a primary source. A low-confidence secondary source says January 2025.
- **Exact date of the September 2024 removals:** not confirmed on FDA's site (FDA's withdrawn table shows no dates). Secondary sources say a Sept 20, 2024 notice with effect around Sept 27, 2024.
- **Sermorelin and gonadorelin:** no primary FDA source was found for their approved-drug-component or USP-monograph basis. Check the USP-NF and Drugs@FDA "discontinued" listings, and confirm with the dispensing pharmacy.
- **Selank dosage forms:** the nominated forms for selank, LL-37 and melanotan II were not retrieved.
- **CagriSema and retatrutide:** current FDA review status is not confirmed from Novo, Lilly or FDA primary sources.

## What happened in April 2026 (Category 2 removals)?

### Takeaway
On **April 15, 2026**, FDA announced in Federal Register notice **2026-07361** that it would remove **12 peptides** from 503A Category 2 because their nominators withdrew the nominations, effective about **April 22, 2026**. It also scheduled PCAC review: 7 peptides in July 2026 and 5 before the end of February 2027. Removal **did not** place any peptide in Category 1 or on the 503A bulks list. FDA's own list says they are now simply "nominated but withdrawn."

### Cited Findings
- **Announcement:** April 15, 2026; removal "after 7 days," about April 22, 2026. The mechanism was nominators withdrawing their nominations. — [Orrick, April 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/FDA-Announces-Removal-of-12-Peptides-from-Category-2-and-Schedules-PCAC-Meetings)

The FDA documents are the [Federal Register public-inspection notice 2026-07361](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-07361.pdf), the [republished interim 503A list](https://www.fda.gov/media/94155/download) and the [Interim Policy guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/174456/download). — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-peptides-do-not-compound-list-update-2026/)

- **PCAC assignments:**
  - July 23–24, 2026: BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTs-C, emideltide, semax, epitalon (each as free base and acetate). Docket FDA-2025-N-6895.
  - Before end of February 2027: LL-37, GHK-Cu, dihexa acetate, melanotan II, PEG-MGF. No public docket yet.

### Inferences
- These 12 now sit in a "no category" limbo. They have no Category 2 safety designation, but they also lack the Category 1 enforcement-discretion shield. Compounding them is technically outside 503A today.
- The 12 removals were nominator-driven. Combined with Kennedy's public preview (see the HHS section), the withdraw-then-re-review sequence looks like a coordinated path to PCAC review rather than an FDA safety finding.

### Gaps
- The full text of FR notice 2026-07361 could not be fetched (federalregister.gov blocked automated access). Its formal publication date may differ from April 15 by a day.
- Some early coverage named MK-677 (ibutamoren) instead of PEG-MGF as the twelfth substance. FDA's own page shows ibutamoren **still in Category 2** and PEG-MGF withdrawn, so the MK-677 version is wrong. — [FDA 503A list PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/94155/download)

## What did PCAC vote on in July 2026, and has FDA acted?

### Takeaway
On **July 23–24, 2026**, a newly reconstituted PCAC voted to recommend **6 of 7** peptides for the 503A bulks list, overriding unanimous negative recommendations from FDA staff. Emideltide (DSIP) failed. Since then FDA has **not** amended the bulks list or Category 1. As of Oct 20, 2026, the only follow-on action is the **Oct 14, 2026** submission of an "Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides" to OMB, which has not been issued.

## Which peptides are in Category 1, which remain in Category 2, and which are on the final 503A bulks list?

### Takeaway
On the May 14, 2026 list (still current), **Category 1** contains only a few peptide or peptide-adjacent items: **GHK-Cu (except injectable), glutathione, NAD/NADH and vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP)**. **Category 2** has six substances, and the only peptide among them is **kisspeptin-10**. **No research peptide is on the final 503A bulks list.**

### Cited Findings
- **Category 1 (May 14, 2026):** includes GHK-Cu (except for injectable routes), glutathione, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD), NADH and vasoactive intestinal peptide. Sermorelin, gonadorelin, thymosins, BPC-157 and other research peptides are **not** listed. — [FDA 503A list PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/94155/download)
- **Category 2 (May 14, 2026):** cesium chloride, domperidone, germanium sesquioxide, ibutamoren mesylate, **kisspeptin-10**, and quinacrine HCl for intrauterine use. — [FDA 503A list PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/94155/download)
- **Category 3 (peptide-relevant):** GHRP-2, GHRP-6, mechano growth factor (MGF, non-pegylated), thymulin acetate, cibinetide (ARA-290), secretin human 99%, and beta-NAD disodium salt trihydrate. — [FDA 503A list PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/94155/download)
- **503B-only Category 2 (outsourcing facilities):** ipamorelin acetate, GHRP-2 (injectable/nasal) and GHRP-6 remain there (added Sept 29, 2023). — [FDA safety-risks page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)
- **Final 503A bulks list:**
  - A February 2019 final rule placed six substances on the list.
  - A September 2019 proposed rule would add five more and decline 26.
  - FDA continues rolling rulemaking.

  None of these involve the research peptides. — [FDA 503A page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/bulk-drug-substances-used-compounding-under-section-503a-fdc-act); [2019 final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2019/02/19/2019-02367/list-of-bulk-drug-substances-that-can-be-used-to-compound-drug-products-in-accordance-with-section)
- **Category 1 cut-off:** FDA will not place substances nominated on or after Jan 7, 2025 into the categories. Category 1 substances stay covered until FDA decides on listing or removes them. — [FDA 503A page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/bulk-drug-substances-used-compounding-under-section-503a-fdc-act)

### Inferences
- **The January 2025 rule limits new Category 1 additions.** Fresh nominations cannot simply land in Category 1 under the existing guidance. That may explain why HHS is pursuing a separate "interim access policy" document rather than adding peptides to Category 1. This is inference only. McDermott and Orrick both described Category 1 placement as one possible bridge.
- **Low-confidence sources are wrong on Category 1.** Vendor and blog claims that sermorelin, gonadorelin or ipamorelin are "Category 1" are contradicted by FDA's own list.

### Gaps
- The text of the 21 CFR 216.23 bulks list was not re-pulled from eCFR. Its contents are well established as non-peptide substances.

## What did the 2023 Category 2 placements cover, and how has that list changed?

### Takeaway
Reconstructed from FDA's own tables, the 2023-era 503A Category 2 peptide group was about **18 peptides**:
- AOD-9604, BPC-157, LL-37, CJC-1295, dihexa, emideltide/DSIP, epitalon, injectable GHK-Cu, ipamorelin, kisspeptin-10, KPV, PEG-MGF, melanotan II, MOTs-C, selank, semax, thymosin alpha-1 and TB-500
- plus ibutamoren (a non-peptide growth hormone secretagogue)

Five were withdrawn in September 2024 (AOD-9604, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, thymosin alpha-1, selank) and went to PCAC in late 2024 with strongly negative votes, except selank, which was never reviewed. Twelve were withdrawn in April 2026. **Only kisspeptin-10 remains.**

### Cited Findings
- **FDA's "nominated but withdrawn" table** (formerly Category 2) lists 17 peptides: AOD-9604, BPC-157, cathelicidin LL-37, CJC-1295, dihexa acetate, emideltide (DSIP), epitalon, GHK-Cu (injectable), ipamorelin acetate, KPV, PEG-MGF, melanotan II, MOTs-C, selank acetate (TP-7), semax, thymosin-alpha 1, and thymosin beta-4 fragment (TB-500). Kisspeptin-10 (added **Sept 29, 2023**) and ibutamoren (503A, Sept 29, 2023) remain in Category 2. — [FDA safety-risks page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)
- **Placement dates:** FDA placed "more than a dozen peptides, including BPC-157" in Category 2 in 2023. — [Holland & Knight, Oct 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/peptides-at-a-crossroads-what-wellness-providers-need-to-know). AP described it as "nearly 20 peptides" under the Biden administration. — [AP via WTOP](https://wtop.com/national/2026/04/fda-to-weigh-easing-limits-on-unproven-peptides-favored-by-rfk-jr-and-other-maha-figures/)
- **September 2024:** AOD-9604, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, thymosin alpha-1 and selank were removed from Category 2 after nominators withdrew, effective about Sept 27, 2024. — (secondary: [Lexology summary via search](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=2e55b76a-3173-4e04-beda-bf021202f18d); [Peptide Journal timeline](https://www.peptidejournal.org/legal/fda-peptide-regulation-complete-2025-2026-timeline), LOW-CONFIDENCE and internally inconsistent)
- **PCAC Oct 29, 2024:**
  - Ipamorelin free base 0–12–1; ipamorelin acetate 0–12–1
  - Kisspeptin-10 0–11
  - Ibutamoren 1–13
  - L-theanine 1–12

  — [FDA minutes](https://www.fda.gov/media/185412/download)
- **PCAC Dec 4, 2024:**
  - CJC-1295 free base 0–13; acetate 1–12; DAC free base, DAC acetate and DAC TFA each 0–13
  - AOD-9604 0–12 (group vote)
  - Thymosin alpha-1 free base 4–17; acetate 4–17

  — [FDA minutes](https://www.fda.gov/media/185642/download)
- **April 2026:** 12 more were withdrawn (see the April section). — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-peptides-do-not-compound-list-update-2026/)

### Inferences
- **2024 group:** FDA has not issued a final rule excluding CJC-1295, ipamorelin, AOD-9604 or thymosin alpha-1. However, the lopsided 2024 PCAC "no" votes make them poor candidates for near-term access. Unless the new committee or HHS revisits them, they are the least likely to be covered by the interim policy.
- **Selank:** its status is pure limbo. It is in no category, has had no PCAC review, and is not compoundable.

### Gaps
- The original 2023 Category 2 placement dates for each peptide (e.g., BPC-157's exact date) were not captured. FDA's withdrawn table omits dates.

## 2026 FDA warning letters to compounders or telehealth companies over peptides

### Takeaway
2026 enforcement has targeted **"research use only" (RUO) online peptide sellers** (five letters on Aug 24, 2026), not 503A pharmacies compounding the 12 withdrawn peptides. Two related signals matter for telehealth:
- A **Sept 18, 2026** letter questioned prescriptions generated through preselected options on third-party telehealth platforms.
- The **glutathione** endotoxin cluster.

### Cited Findings
- **Aug 24, 2026:** CDER sent warning letters to Peak Performance Peptides, Royal Peptides, NuScience Peptides, Peptide Partners and TXP Innovations (Tex Peptides). The letters:
  - allege unapproved new drugs
  - reject "research use only" disclaimers where marketing showed intended human use (e.g., bacteriostatic water bundled with a "peptide calculator")
  - name retatrutide, survodutide, mazdutide, tesamorelin, SS-31/elamipretide, PT-141/bremelanotide and bacteriostatic water

  — [ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/ruo-ined-five-peptide-vendors-learn-research-use-only-not-legal-strategy); [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/fda-balances-peptide-policy-with-enforcement-against-unlawful-peptide-sellers); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/peptides-at-a-crossroads-what-wellness-providers-need-to-know)
- **March 2026:** a warning letter in which FDA relied on website evidence despite RUO disclaimers. — [D.J. Holt Law](https://djholtlaw.com/regulatory-alert-the-legal-status-of-bpc-157-in-compounding-and-clinical-practice/) (recipient not verified)
- **Sept 18, 2026:** FDA warning letter to a compounder over GLP-1 products with added B12 or niacinamide. It also questioned prescriptions generated through **preselected options on third-party telehealth platforms**. — [Holland & Knight, Oct 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/peptides-at-a-crossroads-what-wellness-providers-need-to-know)
- **Glutathione cluster (Aug–Sept 2026):**
  - FDA warned compounders not to use dietary-supplement-grade glutathione for injectables.
  - Medisca lot #229536 was implicated; Medisca notified customers on Aug 20, 2026.
  - The CDC reported 25+ ill in at least 4 states, with hospitalizations.
  - Pharmacy recalls followed, e.g., Centric Compounding (Houston).

  — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-reminds-compounders-not-use-dietary-supplement-grade-glutathione-injectables); [Healio, Sept 17, 2026](https://www.healio.com/news/primary-care/20260917/cdc-investigating-illnesses-tied-to-glutathione-injections)
- **Retatrutide sellers:** FDA has sent 14 warning letters since 2024; as of May 2026, 11 recipients were still advertising unapproved peptides. — [Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/news/hospitalizations-enforcement-gaps-influencers-tied-to-unapproved-retatrutide-products/) (advocacy source)

### Inferences
- FDA's current enforcement posture is "tolerate the pharmacy pathway while it is under review, hit RUO grey-market sellers." A telehealth business is most exposed through:
  - RUO sourcing
  - templated or preselected prescribing
  - non-pharma-grade API

### Gaps
- No 2026 warning letter to a 503A pharmacy or telehealth company **specifically for compounding BPC-157 or another withdrawn peptide** was found. The FDA warning-letter database was not queried directly, so this is not conclusive.

## Statements from HHS leadership (2025–2026) on peptide access

### Takeaway
HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has openly championed peptide access and said he has used peptides himself. He previewed the reclassification in February 2026, before FDA acted in April. FDA career staff opposed every listing.

### Cited Findings
- **Feb 27, 2026:** Kennedy announced that many Category 2 peptides would be considered for reclassification to Category 1. — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/bulk-list-bound-pcac-backs-majority-of-peptides-in-two-day-public-meeting/); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/peptides-at-a-crossroads-what-wellness-providers-need-to-know)
- **On The Joe Rogan Experience** (exact date not confirmed; reported Feb–Apr 2026): "I'm a big fan of peptides… I've used them myself and with really good effect on a couple of injuries." He also said gray-market product is "very, very substandard." — [AP via WTOP, Apr 15, 2026](https://wtop.com/national/2026/04/fda-to-weigh-easing-limits-on-unproven-peptides-favored-by-rfk-jr-and-other-maha-figures/). Frier Levitt says he expected FDA to act on about 14 peptides. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-peptides-do-not-compound-list-update-2026/)
- **Apr 15, 2026:** Kennedy confirmed the removal of 12 peptides from Category 2. — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/bulk-list-bound-pcac-backs-majority-of-peptides-in-two-day-public-meeting/)
- **On the reconstituted PCAC:** HHS spokesperson Emily Hilliard said all members "underwent the same ethics review and vetting process." — [PharmExec](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/fda-votes-loosen-restrictions-four-peptides)
- **Critic:** Peter Lurie (Center for Science in the Public Interest): "Everybody knows the outcome that the secretary wants." — [AP via WTOP](https://wtop.com/national/2026/04/fda-to-weigh-easing-limits-on-unproven-peptides-favored-by-rfk-jr-and-other-maha-figures/)

### Inferences
- Strong political backing makes some form of interim access for the six PCAC-recommended peptides likely in late 2026 or early 2027. Any such access rests on enforcement discretion and could be reversed by a later administration or a court. Holland & Knight flags the 2028 election.

### Gaps
- No direct statement from FDA Commissioner Makary on peptides was found.
- The venue and date of the Rogan remarks were not confirmed from a primary transcript. Reports describe the "big fan" remark as late February 2026.
