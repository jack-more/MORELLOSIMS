# US Regulatory Landscape for $ZUMA (commerce utility token, referral payouts, governance voting) — as of Oct 17, 2026

Not legal advice. Status labels used throughout: **[ENACTED]** = statute in force; **[FINAL/BINDING]** = final rule or court ruling; **[INTERPRETIVE]** = agency interpretation or staff view, not law; **[PROPOSED]** = proposal or pending bill; **[MEMORY]** = a primary-source URL cited from background knowledge and not re-fetched in this session (verify before relying on it).

Context: ZUMA is a small Solana shop selling research peptides labeled "for laboratory research use only, not for human or veterinary use". It has a $ZUMA token with the following features:
- holders get member pricing;
- referral rewards are 10% of a referred order's net value, times a multiplier based on holdings, capped at 30%, paid in USDC or store credit;
- members vote on which factory makes each lot, and get extra discounts for voting streaks;
- there is no revenue share and no buyback promise.

## 1. Securities law: Howey, the SEC's 2025–2026 posture, legislation, and how ZUMA's features score

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 there is still **no enacted federal statute defining a "utility token"**.
- The governing law is still *Howey*, now read through the binding-on-staff but non-statutory **SEC–CFTC joint interpretive release of March 17, 2026**. That release puts "digital tools" (membership, access and credential tokens) outside the definition of a security.
- The **Regulation Crypto Assets** safe harbor and exemptions are only **proposed**: issued Aug 18, 2026, with comments due Oct 20, 2026.
- The **CLARITY Act** market-structure bill **failed cloture in the Senate on Sept 15, 2026 (49–50)** and is stalled.
- The **GENIUS Act** (stablecoins) is **enacted** (July 18, 2025) but takes effect no later than **Jan 18, 2027**, and its implementing rules are still mostly proposals.

ZUMA's consumptive design (member pricing, no revenue share, no buyback) fits the "digital tool" category well. The **holdings-based referral multiplier** is the feature most likely to draw an investment-contract argument, because it pays more USDC the more tokens you hold. Even so, *how ZUMA markets the token* remains the deciding factor.

### Inferences (applied to ZUMA)
- **Member pricing and voting-streak discounts** look like "membership/access rights" and "functional instruments", which is the digital-tool category. They are consumptive, and each benefit is redeemed by the holder's own purchase. The Fuse no-action letter reasoning ("earn for their own consumption") supports this.
- **No revenue share and no buyback promise** removes the two features regulators and courts most often cite as profit expectation. Munchee and the collectibles discussion both flag revenue-sharing and buyback or scarcity promises. Keep any treasury token purchases discretionary, unannounced and never framed as price support. Better still, don't do them at all.
- **Holdings-based referral multiplier (10% × multiplier, capped at 30%, paid in USDC):** this is the weakest point.
  - *Argument against a security:* the payout comes from the referrer's own efforts (bringing in buyers), not from "the efforts of others". It is also capped.
  - *Argument for a security:* the larger the bag, the larger the USDC cash flow per referral. That invites buying tokens to raise a cash-yield rate, which looks like an investment rationale, and a plaintiff or regulator could frame it as a holding-linked return.
  - *Mitigations:*
    - tie the multiplier to verified *purchase history* or *non-transferable membership tier* rather than token balance;
    - or pay the multiplied portion only in store credit;
    - or cap the multiplier low and never market it as "earn more by holding more".
- **Governance (factory selection per lot)** is operational and consumptive, and involves no treasury or profit control. It does not by itself create profit expectation. The separate risk is DAO partnership liability; see Section 6.
- Under the March 2026 release, the **marketing/representations analysis** is central. Website, X posts, Discord and referrer posts are all "recognized channels" that can create investment-contract representations.
- **Regulation Crypto Assets** is designed for tokens sold *with* an investment contract. A consumptive token that is never sold for fundraising likely does not need it. If ZUMA ever sells tokens to raise capital, the $5M/4-year startup exemption could become relevant once it is adopted.

## 2. CFTC/commodity treatment; FinCEN, money transmission, state licensing, OFAC

### Takeaway
- The CFTC co-signed the March 2026 release. A non-security token is generally a "digital commodity", which brings CFTC anti-fraud and anti-manipulation jurisdiction but no registration for a spot merchant token.
- Under FinCEN's 2019 guidance, a **merchant that accepts crypto for its own goods is a "user", not a money transmitter**. Paying referral rewards from ZUMA's own funds for its own business purposes is generally payment of an expense, not transmission.
- **State licensing matters more:** California's DFAL licensing became mandatory **July 1, 2026**. It has a merchant-acceptance exemption, but its application to rewards programs is unclear.
- OFAC compliance is strict liability. Screening wallets is a baseline control.

### Cited Findings
- **CFTC role in the March 17, 2026 joint release:** the CFTC joined to give complementary guidance under the Commodity Exchange Act, and "digital commodities" are not securities. [INTERPRETIVE] — [Dechert](https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2026/3/sec-s-crypto-framework--the-new-token-taxonomy.html); [GT Law](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/3/sec-clarifies-status-of-crypto-assets-under-federal-securities-laws-signals-potential-exemptive-and-safe-harbor-framework)
- **FinCEN 2019 CVC guidance (FIN-2019-G001, May 9, 2019)** [INTERPRETIVE] [MEMORY] — [FinCEN guidance](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf)
  - A person who obtains convertible virtual currency to purchase goods or services is a "user", not an MSB.
  - Money transmission means accepting value from one person and transmitting it to another person or location.
  - Exemptions exist where transmission is integral to providing a non-money-transmission good or service.
- **FinCEN/OFAC PPSI rule:** proposed Apr 8, 2026 [PROPOSED]. It adds permitted payment stablecoin issuers to the BSA "financial institution" definition and carves them out of the MSB definition. Travel Rule thresholds of $3,000 would apply to payment stablecoins. It applies to *issuers* such as Circle, not to merchants. — [Covington, Apr 2026](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/04/fincen-and-ofac-propose-aml-cft-and-sanctions-framework-for-permitted-payment-stablecoin-issuers-five-things-to-know); [FinCEN NPRM PDF](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-04/PPSI-AMLCFT-NPRM.pdf)
- **GENIUS Act yield prohibition** (enacted text; [congress.gov S.1582](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582)) [MEMORY on section detail]: permitted payment stablecoin *issuers* may not pay holders interest or yield solely for holding the stablecoin. A merchant paying USDC as a referral commission is not issuer yield. The Senate market-structure fight over third-party "rewards" on stablecoins is unresolved (CLARITY stalled).
- **FinCEN enforcement environment:** FinCEN Alert FIN-2026-Alert007 (Oct 1, 2026) on the ruble-backed stablecoin A7A5 said more than 180 entities had processed A7A5 transactions. — [FinCEN Alert](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-10/FinCEN-Alert-A7-Network.pdf)
- **California DFAL** [ENACTED]
  - The licensing requirement became operative **July 1, 2026**. DFPI began accepting applications **Mar 9, 2026**.
  - Covered businesses without a filed application by July 1 must stop serving California residents.
  - **Merchants that accept digital financial assets as payment for goods/services are exempt.**
  - One source reports that SB 97 (urgency measure, effective June 30, 2026) changed DFAL's scope (unconfirmed).
  - — [Baker McKenzie, Mar 9, 2026](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2026/03/09/californias-crypto-licensing-era-begins-dfpi-commences-acceptance-of-dfal-applications-on-march-9th/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/cannabis/californias-digital-asset-license-deadline-threatens-disruption-2); [Jenner](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/client-alerts/its-april-do-you-know-where-your-california-digital-assets-license-is); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/358379/california-begins-enforcing-state-level-crypto-licensing-with-dfal)
- **OFAC Sanctions Compliance Guidance for the Virtual Currency Industry (Oct 2021)** [INTERPRETIVE] [MEMORY]: it recommends risk-based programs including geolocation/IP blocking and wallet screening. Sanctions liability is strict. — [OFAC guidance PDF](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/913571/download?inline)

### Inferences
- **Accepting USDC or SOL at checkout through Solana Pay:** this is merchant-acceptance territory, so no MSB registration is likely. Avoid anything that looks like holding customer balances redeemable for crypto, or converting or transferring value *for* customers.
- **Store credit** that is closed-loop, non-cashable and usable only at ZUMA is the lowest-risk payout. Under FinCEN's prepaid-access rules, closed-loop is generally excluded below thresholds. Not verified this session.
- **USDC referral payouts:** these are paid from ZUMA's own treasury as a marketing expense, so they are not transmission. However, any feature letting users *withdraw* accumulated credit as USDC, or move balances to third parties, starts to look like stored value or transmission. That is exactly where state regimes like DFAL and money-transmitter laws bite.
- **Screening:** screen payout wallets against the OFAC SDN list (Chainalysis/TRM free oracle or API) before every USDC payout, and block sanctioned jurisdictions at checkout. Note that Circle can freeze USDC at blacklisted addresses (Circle USDC terms; not verified this session).

- Whether California DFAL's merchant exemption covers *paying out* rewards is unresolved.
- The SB 97 changes were not confirmed.

## 3. FTC: endorsements/affiliates, deceptive and health claims, referral/pyramid risk

### Takeaway
- **Referrers are endorsers.** ZUMA is liable for their undisclosed material connections and for any health or dosing claims they make.
- A **single-level** referral commission paid on genuine retail purchases is not a pyramid. Multi-level payouts, or rewards contingent on recruiting rather than real customer sales, are the classic FTC chain-referral pattern, and the FTC has sued crypto versions of that pattern.
- The FTC's 2025 Earnings Claim Rule and MLM proposals remain **proposals**.

### Cited Findings
- **Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255), revised June 29–30, 2023** [INTERPRETIVE guides; enforced through FTC Act §5] [MEMORY]:
  - material connections must be clearly and conspicuously disclosed, including commissions, free product and token rewards;
  - advertisers are liable for endorsers' deceptive claims and must monitor them.
  - — [FTC press release, June 30, 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/federal-trade-commission-announces-updated-advertising-guides-combat-deceptive-reviews-endorsements)
- **Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465)** [FINAL rule, civil penalties]: finalized Aug 14, 2024, effective Oct 21, 2024. It bans fake reviews and undisclosed insider reviews, and bars buying reviews conditioned on sentiment. [MEMORY] — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)
- **Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022)** [INTERPRETIVE] [MEMORY]: health claims require "competent and reliable scientific evidence", generally high-quality randomized controlled trials on the actual product. Disclaimers cannot cure a misleading net impression. — [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)
- **FTC crypto chain-referral case (Mar 2018)** [court TRO, then settlements]
  - The FTC sued promoters of Bitcoin Funding Team, My7Network and Jetcoin (Dluca, Gatto, Pinkston, Chandler).
  - Participants paid in bitcoin or litecoin for the right to recruit others and earn crypto recruitment rewards. The FTC said there was no product or service, and revenue came only from recruitment.
  - The court froze assets. A later report describes more than $470K in refunds.
  - — [FTC complaint PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/cases/dluca_-_bitcoint_funding_team_complaint.pdf); [FTC consumer alert](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2018/03/bitcoin-online-chain-referral-illegal-scheme?page=0); [Baker McKenzie](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2018/03/16/u-s-ftc-shuts-down-promoters-of-deceptive-cryptocurrency-schemes/)
- **Business Guidance Concerning Multi-Level Marketing (Jan 2018)** [INTERPRETIVE] [MEMORY]: the key question is whether compensation is driven by real retail sales to genuine users or by recruitment and participants' own purchases. Pay-to-play elements raise risk. — [FTC MLM guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guidance-concerning-multi-level-marketing)
- **Earnings claims proposals** [PROPOSED]: on **Jan 13, 2025** the FTC proposed a new Earnings Claim Rule and changes to the Business Opportunity Rule covering MLM earnings claims, with civil penalties and refunds. Two commissioners dissented. **Final status was not found.** — [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/ftc-proposes-changes-to-deter-deceptive-earnings-claims-by-multilevel-marketers-and-money-making-opportunity-sellers/); [Social Selling News](https://socialsellingnews.com/?p=1370)

### Inferences
- ZUMA's **10% single-level referral on net order value**, paid on real third-party purchases, fits the "retail sales" model. Risk rises if any of the following happens:
  - referrers earn on their *own* purchases;
  - payouts go multi-tier ("referrals of referrals");
  - **holding more $ZUMA, which costs money, is required to earn higher commission rates**. That is a pay-for-higher-commission feature a regulator could analogize to pay-to-play;
  - marketing emphasizes "income" or "passive earnings".
- **Disclosure:** every referrer post needs a clear "#ad / I earn a commission and token rewards from ZUMA" disclosure. ZUMA should publish referrer rules and monitor posts.
- **Referrer content is ZUMA's content** for FTC (and FDA) purposes. Referrers who make dosing or benefit claims ("BPC-157 healed my tendon") create deceptive health-claim exposure and FDA intended-use evidence at the same time.

- I found no FTC action from 2024–2026 specifically targeting a crypto *merchant* referral program.
- I did not confirm whether the FTC finalized or withdrew the 2025 Earnings Claim Rule. A June 16, 2026 item notes that the FTC reminded marketers to substantiate earnings claims. — [JD Supra MLM news](https://www.jdsupra.com/law-news/mlm-direct-sales/health-law/communications-media-law/)

## 4. Research peptides: FDA/DOJ enforcement 2024–2026, BPC-157 status, payment/platform policies, and why a token adds scrutiny

### Takeaway
FDA enforcement against online "research use only" (RUO) peptide sellers **intensified in 2026**:
- warning-letter batches dated **Mar 31, 2026** (seven letters, GLP-1 analogs such as retatrutide and tirzepatide);

That did **not** make them compoundable or lawful to sell for human use. A July 23–24, 2026 PCAC meeting was set to consider BPC-157 for the 503A bulks list (outcome not found). Mainstream processors such as Stripe restrict peptides and research chemicals.

## 5. Tax and reporting basics

### Takeaway
- **Member discounts** are generally purchase-price reductions, not income.
- **Referral rewards** paid in USDC or tokens are ordinary income to the recipient at fair market value when received, and become the recipient's basis.
- Form **1099-DA** reports *dispositions* by brokers. It started with gross proceeds for 2025 sales and adds basis for covered assets acquired from Jan 1, 2026. Rewards income is generally **not** on the 1099-DA.
- ZUMA as payer may have 1099-MISC/NEC obligations for referral payments above thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- **Staking rewards:** Rev. Rul. 2023-14 (July 31, 2023) treats rewards as gross income when the taxpayer gains dominion and control, at fair market value. [FINAL guidance] [MEMORY] — [IRS Rev. Rul. 2023-14](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rr-23-14.pdf)
- **Rewards and 1099-DA:** referral bonuses, cashback and other rewards create income but are excluded from Form 1099-DA. They flow through 1099-MISC or are self-reported. FMV at receipt becomes basis. — [beancount.io 2026 guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/10/form-1099-da-digital-asset-broker-reporting-2026-crypto-investors-cost-basis-form-8949-guide); [CoinTracking](https://cointracking.info/blog/form-1099-da/) (secondary)
- **1099-DA timing**
  - Gross proceeds are reported for 2025 transactions.
  - Basis is reported for assets acquired on or after Jan 1, 2026 at the same broker (first basis forms early 2027).
  - Stablecoins have a $10,000 de minimis aggregate threshold with special box codes.
  - Sources conflict on the basis start date; one says 2025, which appears wrong.
  - — [beancount.io](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/13/form-1099-da-digital-asset-broker-reporting-crypto-exchanges-wallet-providers-2026-guide) (secondary)
- **DeFi broker rule repealed:** the "DeFi broker" expansion of the broker rules was nullified under the Congressional Review Act (H.J.Res. 25, signed Apr 10, 2025). [ENACTED] [MEMORY] — [congress.gov H.J.Res.25](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-joint-resolution/25)

### Inferences
- **Recipient side:** USDC referral rewards are taxable income to the referrer at receipt. Store credit is probably also income to a referrer who is not the buyer (a commission). A purchaser's own member discount is a price reduction.
- **Payer side:** ZUMA should collect W-9s (or W-8s) and issue 1099-NEC/MISC for referrers above the reporting threshold. The threshold may have changed for 2026 payments (see Gaps). ZUMA itself is a merchant, not a 1099-DA "broker", unless it effects customers' token sales.
- **Voting-streak discounts** are price reductions. A free token airdrop for voting would be income (FMV) to the recipient.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm the 2026 Form 1099-NEC/MISC threshold. It may have been raised from $600 to $2,000 by 2025 tax legislation; verify with IRS.
- I found no IRS guidance specifically on token-gated discounts or loyalty tokens.
- 1099-DA details here come from secondary tax-software blogs, not IRS instructions.

## 6. Geofencing, offshore and foundation structures, governance liability, and their limits

### Takeaway
Offshore foundations (Cayman, BVI, Panama, Swiss) and US geoblocking are common, but they **do not insulate** a US-run business that sells physical products to US customers. US conduct, US customers and VPN-porous geofences all bring US law back in.

Separately, **token-holder governance** creates partnership-liability risk:
- **CFTC v. Ooki DAO (2023)** treated a DAO as an unincorporated association liable for violations;
- ***Samuels v. Lido DAO* (N.D. Cal., Nov 18, 2024)** allowed claims that a DAO is a **general partnership** whose governance participants and active investors may be jointly liable.

### Cited Findings
- ***Samuels v. Lido DAO*** (N.D. Cal., order on motion to dismiss, **Nov 18, 2024**)
  - Lido DAO plausibly a general partnership under California law, with Paradigm, a16z and Dragonfly plausibly general partners because of active governance participation.
  - The court suggested membership could range from founders to "everyone who has voted on a governance proposal or who holds any LDO".
  - "Offers or sells" was construed broadly to cover solicitation on exchanges.
  - — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/292275/california-court-rules-lido-dao-members-can-be-held-liable-under-partnership-laws); [Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/unitedstates/financial-services/1554392/daos-watch-out-federal-court-in-california-decides-a-dao-can-be-a-general-partnership)
- **CFTC v. Ooki DAO:** default judgment in June 2023. The DAO was held liable as an unincorporated association, with fines and a shutdown order; it had been formed partly to evade regulation. [FINAL/BINDING judgment] [MEMORY] — [CFTC press release 8715-23](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8715-23)
- **CFTC order against Polymarket (Jan 2022):** an offshore event-market operator offering to US persons was ordered to wind down non-compliant markets and pay a $1.4M penalty. This shows US jurisdiction over offshore platforms reachable by US users. [FINAL settled order] [MEMORY] — [CFTC press release 8478-22](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8478-22)
- **OFAC** expects geolocation/IP controls as part of a risk-based program. — [OFAC 2021 VC guidance](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/913571/download?inline) [MEMORY]
- **California DFAL** binds anyone doing covered activity with California residents, regardless of where the entity sits. — [Baker McKenzie](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2026/03/09/californias-crypto-licensing-era-begins-dfpi-commences-acceptance-of-dfal-applications-on-march-9th/)

### Inferences
- **For ZUMA, a US geofence is incoherent.** A US-facing peptide store whose token unlocks member pricing *for those same customers* cannot geofence the token from the US without geofencing the business. Offshore token structures are mainly used to sell tokens to investors (Reg S); ZUMA is not doing that.
- **The realistic structure** is a US entity (an LLC or C-corp, possibly with a separate subsidiary for token operations) that keeps the token consumptive and never sells it for fundraising. A non-US foundation adds cost and "evasion" optics without removing FDA, FTC or state exposure for US sales.
- **Governance mitigation:**
  - keep votes **advisory/non-binding**, with the company retaining final decision authority and legal responsibility for the manufacturing choice;
  - or wrap governance in a legal entity (e.g., a Wyoming DAO LLC, Utah DAO Act entity, or Marshall Islands DAO LLC) so voters are not general partners.
  - Votes on supplier selection involve no profit distribution, which weakens partnership "co-ownership for profit" arguments.
  - Rewarding voting with discounts (streaks) is benign. Do not let voters direct treasury or profits.

### Gaps
- I found no later developments in *Samuels v. Lido DAO* after Nov 2024 (amendments, settlement, appeal).
- I found no 2025–2026 cases on geofence adequacy.
- I did not verify current Wyoming or Utah DAO statutes.

## 7. Practical do's and don'ts for marketing copy (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Under the March 2026 release, FTC guides and FDA intended-use doctrine, **words are the main risk lever**. Describe $ZUMA as a membership/access tool; never describe it as an investment. Describe peptides strictly as laboratory reagents, with zero human-use, benefit or dosing content anywhere, including referrer and community content.

### Cited Findings
- **Securities:** investment-contract status turns on attributable, specific representations in websites, white papers and similar channels. — [GT Law](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/3/sec-clarifies-status-of-crypto-assets-under-federal-securities-laws-signals-potential-exemptive-and-safe-harbor-framework). Meme coin and utility precedents fail when marketed for profit. — [SEC meme coin statement](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/staff-statement-meme-coins) [MEMORY]; [Munchee order](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/admin/2017/33-10445.pdf) [MEMORY]
- **FDA:** RUO disclaimers do not override dosing guides, bacteriostatic water bundles or benefit claims. — [ChemVerify](https://www.chemverify.com/learn/fda-research-use-only-enforcement-warning-letters-2026); [FDA letter 735127](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/peak-performance-peptides-735127-08242026)
- **FTC:** disclose material connections; substantiate health claims with RCT-grade evidence; avoid earnings claims. — [FTC Endorsement Guides 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/federal-trade-commission-announces-updated-advertising-guides-combat-deceptive-reviews-endorsements) [MEMORY]; [FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) [MEMORY]; [FTC Bitcoin Funding Team complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/cases/dluca_-_bitcoint_funding_team_complaint.pdf)

### Inferences: do's and don'ts

**DO**
- Use membership and access language: "member pricing", "members-only lots", "a membership key".
- State plainly: "$ZUMA is not an investment; no revenue share, no buybacks, no promise of value; price may go to zero."
- Pay the referral multiplier on purchase-based tiers or in store credit. Disclose commission terms. Require #ad/commission disclosures from referrers, monitor them, and take down non-compliant posts.
- Keep governance votes framed as product and supplier preference polls, with final authority in the company.
- Screen wallets for OFAC sanctions, and keep KYC-lite records (W-9) for referrers receiving USDC.
- Keep RUO language consistent across labels, product pages, checkout attestations and community rules. Moderate the Discord, X and Telegram channels you control for dosing and human-use talk.

**DON'T**
- Don't mention price, "moon", "early", appreciation, APY/yield, "earn passive income", listings or liquidity milestones, token burns framed as scarcity, treasury buybacks, "holders share in growth", or charts.
- Don't market the multiplier as "hold more to earn more". Don't pay referrals on a referrer's own purchases, and don't pay on multiple levels.
- Don't publish or allow dosing, reconstitution guides aimed at humans, before/after content, testimonials, "same as Ozempic" comparisons, or bacteriostatic-water bundles. Don't use health-outcome words such as healing, fat loss, recovery or anti-aging.
- Don't call the offshore entity or geofence a reason the rules don't apply. Don't tout crypto checkout as a way around card-network or processor bans.
- Don't let governance control treasury, pricing profits or distributions.

### Gaps
- No regulator has published a specific "loyalty token marketing" checklist. The do's and don'ts above are synthesized from the sources cited, not quoted from any agency.
